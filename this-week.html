Microsoft SecurityAI Governance

When Agentic AI Runs the Attack

Microsoft attributes a ConnectWise supply-chain compromise to China-aligned STORM-0940, which used the open-source agentic framework ARTEX to accelerate familiar tradecraft - password spray and OAuth app abuse - to machine speed: 2,250+ tenants at 2,900 sign-in attempts per minute, and privileged cloud access in under three hours in several cases. Defender guidance: preserve logs, inventory the four app IDs, review from Oct 15, and don't delete apps first.

Agents Are Your Newest Insiders

Microsoft's case for governing every AI agent as an insider: each gets its own identity, starts with zero standing access, and runs in a sandboxed, independently monitored runtime. Risk is reassessed continuously and permissions adjust in graduated steps — reduce, block writes, narrow, or isolate — via Project Perception, Agent 365, and Entra Agent ID. Discovery now spans 60 local agent types.

Microsoft SecurityAI Governance

Managing Open-Weight Model Risk

Microsoft and NVIDIA argue weight-openness isn't a risk rating — an open-weight model's risk comes from its use case, deployment, and safeguards. Their new joint whitepaper gives a shared five-category taxonomy (cybersecurity, data protection, content, human-AI interaction, bias) so teams speak one language, maps each risk to evaluations (Microsoft Foundry adds groundedness, safety scoring, and automated red teaming), and ties every risk to a working control and an accountable owner — revisited as models and deployments change.

Why the Insurance Workflow Is Still Stuck

Reviewing Code at Agent Speed

Claude writes about 90% of Claude Code, so the team stopped treating human line-by-line reading as the safeguard. Layered automated checks — Claude self-verification, AI review with verifier voting, security review, CI, a merge queue — do most of the reading, and human approval is scaled by a risk tier set from the change.
