How monday.com Became an Agent-First Product

monday.com hit a ceiling sprinkling AI features onto existing workflows. So it rebuilt the platform from the ground up around human-agent collaboration, with Claude at the core. Two months and five million agent interactions later, its team shares five lessons.

So what for you

If you’re weighing how to embed AI in a product or workflow, monday’s lesson is blunt: adopting AI features is not the same as becoming an AI company — the mental-model shift, plus governance and trust, matter more than the technology, and agents work best grounded in live data inside workflows people already use.

The turning point

From ‘AI dust’ to agent-first

More than 250,000 companies — from SMBs to the Fortune 500 — use monday.com to manage work. Its first wave of AI embedded capabilities into the original platform, culminating in a May 2025 “AI month.” Adoption was strong, but the team hit a ceiling: they were building “AI dust,” sprinkling automations onto existing workflows without changing the product’s value proposition. Features could summarize and categorize, but weren’t creating sustained usage.

Adopting AI features is not the same as becoming an AI company. Once we understood that, everything changed.

So monday reimagined the product completely — from a work-management tool into a place where people and agents get work done together, reusing the platform’s existing context, boards, permissions, and governance. Since launching in May 2026, customers have had more than five million agent interactions.

The design

Agents that behave like coworkers

Each monday agent gets a name, an avatar, and its own access permissions, and colleagues assign it work through triggers and mentions — deliberately addressing a pattern where enterprises stall at an AI chat running parallel to where work actually happens. Embedding agents directly into workflows turned agentic AI from an abstract concept into a concrete teammate.

The jobs monday maps for agents span IT ticket triage and knowledge-base upkeep, candidate sourcing and interview scheduling, competitive-intelligence briefings, and chief-of-staff work like meeting prep and turning decisions into tracked tasks.

The lessons

Five things monday learned

One end-to-end example: a marketing team runs a whole campaign inside a single board item — a Strategist Agent turns a brief into structured messaging, a Landing Page Builder (running on Claude Managed Agents in monday’s own environment) generates a page variant, and a Brand Reviewer checks it against guidelines before a human decides to publish or refine. For companies planning a similar rebuild, monday shares five lessons.

Mental model > technology1

Moving teams from “how do we improve the current product?” to “how do we rebuild it for a different future?” took longer than the technical work.

Small teams move faster2

With direction, UX, tech, pricing, and the trust model all in motion at once, small teams with clear ownership stayed close to the detail.

Adoption depends on trust3

Governance, permissions, transparency, and reliability determine whether agents move beyond pilots into production.

Capability needs infrastructure4

Agents perform better grounded in live project data; at enterprise scale the backend has to hold, so monday invested in its data layer.

Build on what already works5

The agent-first rebuild extended monday’s existing promise — a place to drive business outcomes — to a new kind of team member.

The takeaway for builders

Don’t bolt AI onto the workflow — rebuild the workflow around it, then let people assign agents work the way they’d delegate to a coworker. Ground agents in live data and existing governance, and treat trust as a first-class feature.
