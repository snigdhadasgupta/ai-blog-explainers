Zero Data Retention, Without Blind Safety

OpenAI is reaffirming Zero Data Retention for eligible API customers and previewing Private Safety Processing — a way to catch misuse that only shows up across many interactions, without giving OpenAI staff access to the underlying prompts or responses.

The idea in five lines

  • ZDR is the promise — for eligible API customers, OpenAI doesn’t retain prompts or responses after a request, staff can’t review them, and enterprise data isn’t used for training unless customers opt in.
  • Single-interaction safety isn’t enough — the most serious risks show up only across many interactions: repeated probing, coordination across accounts, or an agent that keeps acting after being told to stop.
  • Private Safety Processing extends monitoring — automated systems spot patterns across related interactions without OpenAI personnel seeing the content behind them.
  • The customer keeps the keys — content stays on customer-controlled infrastructure, or on OpenAI storage encrypted with customer-held keys; OpenAI gets only a narrow signal when a risk is flagged.
  • Rolling out in September — in testing with early customers now, with a technical white paper and phased rollout planned for September.
So what for you

If you run regulated or sensitive workloads on a model API, this is the tradeoff to watch: safety monitoring that used to require handing over retained content can now happen without it. Ask your providers whether their safety systems are ZDR-compatible and who holds the encryption keys.

The problem

The riskiest behavior hides across interactions

Existing ZDR-compatible safety systems evaluate each interaction on its own. But harmful intent often becomes visible only when several interactions are viewed together — a bad actor probing safeguards repeatedly, coordinating across accounts, or disguising a threat as routine research. Risk can also build during a long agentic task, for example when a system keeps acting after a user tells it to stop.

Some recent frontier deployments required customers to let their provider retain sensitive content for safety monitoring. For many organizations, that conflicts with their security obligations.

How Private Safety Processing works

Monitor the pattern, never the content

↻ Customers see full alerts and can choose to share content to appeal, clarify, or support an abuse investigation.
Control and enforcement

Alerts you can investigate on your own terms

When something is flagged, customers can investigate using information already in their own systems. If they want to appeal an enforcement decision, clarify legitimate activity, or support an investigation into verified abuse, they can choose to share relevant information with OpenAI — it’s never taken automatically.

One legally-required exception remains: like other frontier providers, OpenAI must report apparent child sexual abuse material, so images flagged as potential CSAM are still retained for manual review even under ZDR.

Who it’s for

Shaped by the most sensitive workloads

OpenAI says Private Safety Processing is being shaped by customers across industries, regions, and company sizes — organizations handling financial records, health data, confidential business plans, and proprietary research, where protecting information is essential to regulatory compliance and competitive advantage. Glean, Databricks, Abridge, and Microsoft are among those cited.

Enterprise AI adoption depends solely on customer control of data… OpenAI’s no-training commitment and ZDR give Glean confidence to build with OpenAI. — Sunil Agrawal, CISO, Glean

What to plan for

Private Safety Processing is in testing with early customers now, with a technical white paper and phased rollout planned for September. If you run ZDR workloads, expect updates on how it interacts with your existing commitments — and decide whether customer-held-key storage or your own infrastructure best fits your obligations.
