How the Designer Who Built Claude Design Uses It

Nate Parrott was the solo designer on Claude Code for VS Code, falling behind engineers who shipped faster than he could. His side project to catch up became Claude Design — a tool for turning half-formed ideas into shareable visuals before anyone commits to building them.

The idea in five lines

  • Claude Design began as a side project by Nate Parrott, the lone designer on Claude Code for VS Code, trying to keep pace with engineers who were suddenly shipping far faster.
  • The unlock: Claude is excellent at HTML — and HTML is a rich visual medium for slides, PDFs, one-pagers, emails, and animations, not just websites.
  • It sits “one click above product design”: a tool for any visual communication whose main job is ideation and getting buy-in — built to your brand.
  • Use Claude Design for early ideation, collaboration, and alignment; use Claude Code for production software. The two round-trip between each other.
  • It’s in beta on Claude Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans.
Why it exists

A designer trying to keep up with his own team

In late 2025, Parrott was the only product designer on Claude Code for VS Code, working with two engineers. After the beta shipped and Opus 4.5 arrived, the team started shipping fast and aggressively — the engineers were delivering far more than before, while he was still working at the pace he always had. He needed a way to catch up.

His first attempts treated Claude like the terminal it lived in: paste output, add screenshots, ask “design this.” The results weren’t good. Then he found the real unlock — Claude is remarkably good at HTML, and HTML is far more than a website format.

Anything you can make in a slide deck, a video file, or a PDF, you can make in a web page — a rich, interactive visual medium.

He gave Claude a split-view playground — chat on the left, live output on the right — then distilled Anthropic’s brand (fonts, colors, assets, principles) into prompts so the output came back on-brand. Product designers picked it up immediately for interactive prototypes: instead of mocking up every screen state and wiring them together by hand, you hand Claude your assets and say “make it work.”

What it became

A medium for any visual communication

At an Anthropic Labs offsite, everyone at an idea-pitch session threw together slides with the prototype — often mid-meeting, right before their turn. That convinced the team to staff it, and Claude Design went from side project to real product. It stopped being a tool for mockups and became a tool for making anything whose job is to communicate.

Make

Decks & one-pagers

Slide decks, landing pages, printable PDFs, emails — presentation-worthy and on-brand, from a prompt and your files.

Move

Prototypes & animations

Click-through prototypes and even animations — visuals that are alive by default rather than static mockups.

Share

A link, every time

Every artifact ships with a link you share the way you’d share a doc — feedback and iteration built in.

Vision improvements compound the value: Opus 5 is better than earlier Opus models at reading charts, diagrams, and screenshots — which makes it powerful paired with Claude Design for turning raw inputs into decks and memos.

Where it fits

Design for ideas; code for production

Claude Design has no image model, so it’s a poor fit for logo design — bring the assets you already have. And if you’re shipping production software, stay in Claude Code. The line is about where in the process you are.

Claude Design

The early, fuzzy front end

Ideation, wireframing, collaboration, and getting buy-in on a direction before anyone commits to building it. Options and starting points, so you never stare at a blank canvas.

Claude Code

The build

Production software and shipping code. Sync a Claude Code prototype into Claude Design to iterate on canvas, or hand a Design prototype back to Code when you’re ready to build.

How to use it well

Ten practices from the person who built it

1

Do the thinking before you prompt

Figure out what you want while away from the computer, so Claude can execute your exact vision when you sit down.

2

Tell Claude what it should look like

Left undirected, Claude picks one of its favorite aesthetics. Specify fonts and colors or give it a moodboard.

3

Turn recurring work into a design system

Upload brand files, logos, decks, and typography once; each new artifact starts from your choices, not a blank slate.

4

Ask for ten options, then remix

Most won’t be good, and that’s fine. “I like B and a little of D — give me five riffs that smoosh them.”

5

Sketch what you can’t describe

If a layout is in your head with no words for it, draw it on paper and upload a photo.

6

Point and talk

Instead of writing a paragraph to identify an element, click it and dictate into the comment box.

7

Wireframe first when fidelity doesn’t matter

Wireframes are faster and keep Claude on structure, not visuals — a great way to try many ideas quickly.

8

Make the last mile manual

Rearrange, resize, and recolor with direct-edit tools. They cost no tokens and small tweaks are better eyeballed.

9

Give Claude your real context

Connect GitHub so Claude reuses your components and screens. Web search and MCP work in Design too.

10

Keep working alongside Claude

Don’t wait for a finished result — queue multiple messages or keep talking while Claude works.

Getting started

Claude Design is in beta on Claude Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans. As models get better at building production software, the work that matters most — having good ideas, aligning people, and collecting feedback while an idea is still early — moves earlier in the process. That’s the part Claude Design is built for.
