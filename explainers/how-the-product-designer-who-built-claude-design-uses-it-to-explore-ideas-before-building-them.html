How the Product Designer Who Built Claude Design Uses It

Nate Parrott was the lone designer on Claude Code for VS Code, falling behind engineers who suddenly shipped fast. His fix — treat HTML as a visual medium and let Claude build in it — grew into Claude Design.

The problem

One designer, engineers shipping at AI speed

In fall 2025, Nate Parrott was the only product designer on Claude Code for VS Code, working with two engineers to bring Claude Code’s power into a friendly interface outside the terminal. They shipped the beta at the end of September; when Opus 4.5 arrived in November, the team started shipping fast and aggressively. The engineers were producing far more than before — while Nate was still delivering at his usual pace. He needed a way to catch up.

His first attempt treated design the way Claude Code treats everything: as text. He pasted output into Claude, added screenshots, and asked it to design a feature. The results weren’t good. So for about a month, as a side project, he kept hunting for a way to improve Claude’s design output.

We think of HTML as the format for websites, but it’s also a rich, interactive visual medium: anything you can make in a slide deck, a video file, or a PDF, you can make in a web page.

The breakthrough

Give Claude an HTML playground — and a brand

Eventually Nate found the answer: Claude is really good with HTML. He prompted it to make HTML and gave it a split-view interface — chat on the left, live output on the right. That was useful, but product design applies knowledge of a specific product and brand, so he spent time distilling the essence of Anthropic’s brand — fonts, colors, assets, principles — into prompts. Now the output came back compliant with the brand guide.

He put it into a small internal prototype and shared it. Designers picked it up immediately for interactive prototypes. Traditionally, a click-through prototype means mocking up every state of every screen and wiring them together by hand. Here, you hand Claude your assets and say make it work — and every artifact it delivers has a link you can share like a doc. At an Anthropic Labs offsite, everyone threw together slides with it, often mid-meeting before their turn to present. That session convinced Labs to staff it, and a side project became a real one.

The division of labor

Claude Code ships it; Claude Design shapes it

The two tools aren’t competitors — they’re a round-trip. Sync a prototype started in Claude Code to Claude Design for canvas iteration, or hand off a Design prototype to Code when you’re ready to build. As models get better at building production software, the work that matters most moves earlier: having good ideas, aligning people, and collecting feedback while an idea is still cheap to change.

In daily practice

Ten ways Nate uses Claude Design

The recurring theme: do the thinking before you prompt, direct the aesthetic, and keep the last mile manual.

Figure 2 · Working practices, from prompt to polish
1

Do the thinking before you prompt. Figure out what you want away from the computer — dictate, type on your phone, record a voice note — so Claude executes your exact vision.

2

Tell Claude what it should look like. Left undirected, Claude picks a house aesthetic. Specify fonts and colors, or supply a moodboard.

3

Turn recurring work into a design system. Upload brand files, logos, decks, and type specs, and each artifact starts from your choices.

4

Ask for ten options, then remix. Most won’t be good; one or two will. Then smoosh the winners into five riffs.

5

Sketch what you can’t describe. Draw a layout on paper and upload a photo.

6

Point and talk. Click an element and speak into a comment instead of writing a paragraph to identify it.

7

Wireframe first when fidelity doesn’t matter. Faster, and it keeps Claude on structure so you can try many ideas quickly.

8

Make the last mile manual. Use direct editing for final touches — it costs no tokens, and sizing is better eyeballed.

9

Give Claude your real context. Connect GitHub so it fetches your components and screens; web search and MCP work too.

10

Keep working alongside Claude. Queue multiple messages or keep talking while it works the previous turn.

Newer vision models widen the range: Opus 5 is better at reading charts, diagrams, and screenshots, which makes it powerful paired with Claude Design for presentation-worthy decks and memos.

The philosophy

Make it alive

Nate points designers to Bret Victor’s talk Stop Drawing Dead Fish and its premise: everything we draw should be alive by default. His favorite Claude Design creations are the ones that don’t fit existing boxes — docs with interactive simulations, slide decks that talk to you, diagrams that are also videos, designs that are also their own editors.

Code, specifically HTML, is an amazing medium for creativity, and it’s finally somewhat easy for designers to create with.

Claude Design took its shape because people kept finding uses its creator hadn’t planned. It’s now available in beta on Claude Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans.

Design earlier, decide faster

Use Claude Design for ideation and alignment, Claude Code to build — and let ideas round-trip between them while they’re still cheap to change.
