Building with GPT-6, in Practice

OpenAI’s field guide to GPT-6 is less about raw capability and more about discipline: pick the right model for the job, cache aggressively, describe outcomes instead of micromanaging steps, and give long-running agents a way to be steered mid-flight.

The idea in five lines

  • Three models, three jobs — Astra for hardest reasoning, Sol for near-Astra at a fifth the cost, Luna for fast everyday tasks.
  • Cache before you scale — cached input tokens can cost up to 95% less, and compaction trims context while preserving state.
  • Specify outcomes, not scripts — models now handle nuance, so over-detailed instructions can hurt more than help.
  • Steer work in flight — send corrections mid-turn over WebSocket and use async tool calls so the model keeps working.
  • Measure the right thing — track cost-per-successful-task, latency and success rate before you ship.
So what for you

Before your next model upgrade, instrument cost-per-successful-task — not tokens, not raw latency. It’s the number that tells you whether moving a workload from Astra to Sol, or turning on caching, actually pays off. Then rewrite your prompts to state the goal and the guardrails, and delete the step-by-step scripting.

Why it matters

The hard part moved from prompts to plumbing

As models get better at nuance, the bottleneck shifts from clever prompting to the operational scaffolding around them: which model runs which task, how context is cached and compacted, and how a long-running job is supervised. The guide is a checklist for that scaffolding.

Models have gotten much better at understanding nuance and ambiguity, so overly specific guidance can now hinder results.

What to do next

Pick one workload, define what done means (built, tested, failures handled), and set decision boundaries for what the model may do alone. Turn on caching, then compare cost-per-successful-task across Astra, Sol and Luna.
