Claude in Chrome Is Generally Available

Claude can now work through tasks in your browser — reading pages, clicking, filling forms with your existing logins — and take actions on its own instead of waiting for approval on every step. A safety classifier validates each action before it runs.

The idea in five lines

  • Generally available now — Claude in Chrome ships on every paid Claude plan, graduating from last year's limited pilot to general release.
  • Acts without step approval — Claude auto-approves actions it judges safe, the same mechanism as auto mode in Claude Code; you can switch back to approving manually.
  • Prompt injection is the risk — Hidden instructions in pages, emails, or form fields can redirect an agent to act against you; that threat gated the wider release.
  • Three layered defenses — An attack-trained model, probes that scan tool results before Claude acts, and a classifier that checks each action against your request.
  • Zero successful attacks — With probes plus the classifier, no attacks succeeded against Sonnet 5 or Opus 5 on the current eval; 0.3% against Fable 5, all low-severity.
So what for you

If you are evaluating browser agents, the story here is the safety envelope, not the autonomy. Start Claude on internal dashboards and vendor portals you trust, keep manual approval on for anything that moves money or sends mail, and note that Enterprise admins can limit it to approved domains.

Why it matters

The browser is where the un-connected work lives

Many everyday tools connect to Claude, but plenty don't — internal dashboards, legacy systems, vendor portals. Claude in Chrome reaches those: it can view the page you are on, read and type text, click links, navigate between pages, and fill out forms, all using your existing logins. What changes at general availability is autonomy — Claude no longer needs you to approve every action.

A classifier reviews each action Claude is about to take and checks it against what you originally asked for. If the action doesn't match your request, it's blocked.

The threat model

What prompt injection actually does

In a prompt-injection attack, malicious instructions hide in web content — a page, an email, a form field. You may never see them, but they can redirect the agent to do something you never asked for. Anthropic's own example: you ask Claude to draft replies to your emails, and a hidden instruction in one message tells Claude to forward your other emails to an attacker instead.

Because a browser agent acts with your logins, the blast radius is real. That is why Claude in Chrome ran as a pilot first, while Anthropic hardened its defenses — and why the safeguards below, not the autonomy, are the reason it is now safe to release widely.

Limits & controls

What it doesn't do yet

Claude in Chrome doesn't run on other Chromium browsers or on mobile yet, and you still need the Claude desktop app to work with files on your computer or with other applications. On Enterprise plans, admins manage it in Organization Settings and can limit it to approved domains — see the admin setup guide before rolling it out. The safeguards meaningfully reduce prompt-injection risk but can't eliminate it, so trusted sites are the right place to start.

What to do before you roll it out

Install from the Chrome Web Store, start on trusted internal sites, and keep the manual-approval override in mind for high-impact actions. Enterprise admins should scope it to approved domains and read the admin setup guide first.
