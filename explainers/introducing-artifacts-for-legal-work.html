Ready-to-Use Artifacts for Legal Teams

Claude for Legal now ships ready-to-use artifacts — interactive trackers and dashboards a legal team opens with an example, then adapts with its own documents, connectors, and sharing rules. The model does the first pass; a lawyer applies judgment.

The idea in five lines

  • Artifacts, not prompts — a gallery of task-specific interactive tools (trackers, dashboards) that each open with an example before a team adds its own data.
  • Connectors do the loading — pull in playbooks and research sources so nobody uploads documents by hand, with more than 60 legal connectors added since the May launch.
  • Every output is sourced — each answer links back to the document or law it came from, and sharing supports ethical walls between teams.
  • The workflow is explicit — the professional brings the documents, Claude does the first pass, and a lawyer applies judgment.
  • Partners and public interest — artifacts built by firms and legal-tech vendors, plus access-to-justice tools from nonprofits.
So what for you

Even outside legal, this is the shape to notice: a shareable artifact that opens pre-built with an example beats a blank prompt for getting a team to actually adopt AI. If your group has a recurring review task, the template-plus-connector pattern is what makes it stick.

Why it matters

From blank box to a tool you can hand over

Legal teams asked for two things: simpler setup, and an easier way to work with colleagues. Artifacts answer both. Instead of describing a task from scratch each time, a lawyer opens an artifact that already does it, swaps in their matter, and shares the result — inside or outside the firm — with sharing settings that preserve privacy and ethical walls. The division of labor stays clear: Claude completes a first pass across the documents, and the lawyer brings the judgment.

The professional brings the sources; Claude does the first pass; a lawyer decides. The artifact is the handoff point.

The gallery

Six artifacts that ship on day one

Verify

Cite check

Compares a document with its sources and flags mismatched quotes, wrong page numbers, and claims the source doesn’t support.

Compare

Jurisdiction survey matrix

Lines up a topic — say data-breach notice rules — across states or countries in one table, each cell linked to the law.

Track

Matter board

Turns engagement letters, emails, or spreadsheets into one board of open matters with owner, next step, and next deadline.

Flag

Playbook deviation board

Compares contracts with standard terms, flags differing clauses, and routes them to the exception approver.

Scan

Regulatory horizon scan

Maps upcoming regulations on a timeline by business and location, ranked by impact, with source links.

Extract

Tabular review

Builds a table across contracts for terms like severance or non-competes, linking to the exact language.

Beyond the gallery

Partners, connectors, and access to justice

Legal-tech companies and firms are shipping their own artifacts — an AI Governance Benchmark from Goodwin, a legal-request intake tool from Harvey, an obligations checker from Foley & Lardner, and an audit-ready subpoena-response drafter from PwC. The connector library has grown past 60 since Claude for Legal launched in May, spanning sources like Bloomberg Law, Filevine, Ivo, Legora, and Patlytics.
