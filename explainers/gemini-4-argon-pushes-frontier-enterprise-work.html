So what for you

If a model upgrade is on your roadmap, don’t trust the headline score — re-run it on your own messiest documents. The gains here cluster in multi-step analysis and report drafting, so that is where a swap is most likely to pay off, and where you should measure it.

What Box tested

A benchmark built from real deliverables

Most model leaderboards reward quick recall. Box’s Complex Work Eval does the opposite: it gives an agent a bundle of genuine enterprise content — spreadsheets, PDFs, presentations, images — asks for a finished deliverable, then grades that output against weighted rubrics, running each task multiple times to smooth out luck. It is a test of whether a model can carry a messy, multi-step job all the way through.

Gemini 4 Argon scored 85% against 71% for Gemini 3.1 Pro — and the gap is widest on the document-heavy work enterprises actually hand to agents.

The headline matters less than the shape of the improvement: the model stopped leaving tasks half-done. It read a drug-interaction network straight from a diagram instead of a neat adjacent table, reviewed every imaging study in a batch rather than a sample, found all the required approvers in a legal guideline, and revised an ICU medication plan to stay consistent with its own diagnosis. Box also reports a tighter worst-case run time.

How to read it

Method, and the caveat that comes with it

The eval’s design is what makes the numbers worth a second look. Tasks are scored against weighted rubrics tied to the finished deliverable, not a single right answer, and each is run several times so a lucky pass doesn’t flatter the model. That rewards exactly the behavior that breaks real agent deployments: reading every input, not just the convenient one; staying internally consistent across a long chain of steps; and finishing rather than trailing off. The failure examples Box cites — reading a diagram instead of a table, reviewing a whole batch, aligning a plan with its own diagnosis — are all completeness-and-consistency failures, which is where production agents most often disappoint.

Two caveats keep this honest. First, these are Box’s own results on Box’s own benchmark and Google’s new model — useful as directional evidence that the ceiling moved, not as a procurement decision. Second, Gemini 4 Argon is “coming soon” to Box AI, so availability isn’t live yet. The move that pays off is to take your own hardest, most document-heavy tasks and re-run them when the model lands, scoring the finished output the way Box does rather than trusting the overall percentage.

What to do when it ships

Shortlist the two or three workflows where agents today quietly stop short — multi-file analysis, report drafting, batch review in a regulated process — and keep a small eval set of your own finished-deliverable tasks ready. When Gemini 4 Argon reaches Box AI, measure the lift on those before you roll it out broadly.
