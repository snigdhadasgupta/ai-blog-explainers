Enterprise Frontier Safeguards

Regulated enterprises faced a dilemma: frontier models need retained data to catch cross-session misuse, but compliance rules made data retention a non-starter. Anthropic’s answer, built with 100+ customers, is to keep the monitoring data in the customer’s own cloud — the privacy of zero retention, without the blind spot.

The idea in five lines

  • Privacy and detection, together — EFS combines the privacy of zero data retention with state-of-the-art monitoring for misuse, instead of forcing a choice between them.
  • Your cloud, your keys — monitoring data lives in the customer’s own cloud account (S3, Azure Blob, or Google Cloud Storage), under their encryption keys, access policies, and audit logs.
  • No Anthropic human in the loop — automated systems flag serious misuse and send those signals straight to the customer; the customer’s own cleared staff decide what happens next.
  • Built with the buyers — designed with 100+ enterprises spanning a quarter of the Fortune 100, every US global systemically important bank, and virtually every regulated industry.
  • Opt-in and free from Anthropic — storage, keys, and automated review are each opt-in; Anthropic doesn’t charge, and it rolls out in phases starting later this fall.
So what for you

If data-retention rules have kept your organization off frontier models, EFS is the unlock to raise with your security and compliance teams — the architecture keeps logs in infrastructure you already control. Start by mapping which of your existing cloud environments (AWS, Azure, GCP) would hold the monitoring data.

The dilemma it solves

Why “just delete the data” wasn’t safe enough

The most sophisticated misuse — fraud rings, autonomous cyberattacks, stolen enterprise credentials — unfolds across many sessions and accounts. Analyzing each interaction in isolation and instantly discarding it misses the pattern. That’s why Anthropic introduced 30-day retention with Fable 5 (never to train on enterprise data, which it says it has never done without permission, but to correlate signals over time).

Regulated enterprises understood the security value but often couldn’t live with retention. EFS resolves the standoff by moving where the data sits: it stays inside the customer’s environment, so monitoring can still look across time and accounts without the model provider holding the logs.

Our logs stay in a Wells-managed environment under Wells-managed keys. We keep custody of our data while Anthropic operates the detection. — Wells Fargo CISO

Designed with customers

Three concerns, three controls

Monitoring

Customers control review

Enterprises already monitor for insider risk and want to uplevel it for agents. When automated systems spot a pattern, the signals go directly to the customer to review — on their own terms and standards.

Data storage

Stored where you already trust

Adding a new “trusted data vendor” means contract updates and customer notifications. EFS instead lets data live in the customer’s existing cloud, under their keys, access policies, and audit logging.

Human review

Your reviewer, not theirs

For privileged legal material, non-public info, or drug-safety data, the person clearing a flag must be one of the customer’s own cleared staff. EFS requires no Anthropic human review.

How it works

The data path, end to end

Figure 1 · Customer-owned monitoring loop
1

Activity logged

Traffic and activity data are written to the customer’s own cloud account — S3, Azure Blob, or GCS.

2

Automated analysis

Anthropic’s systems analyze a rolling window for serious misuse: offensive cyber or bio capability, stolen credentials.

3

Signals to customer

Flags route directly to the customer — not to Anthropic reviewers — with the context to act.

4

Customer decides

The customer’s cleared team confirms real misuse, clears false positives, and takes the response.

↻ Customer-owned storage, customer-managed keys, and automated review are each opt-in — none change model behavior, pricing, or rate limits.

The controls work the same whether you reach Claude directly or via a cloud partner. EFS will be supported on Claude Code, Claude Enterprise, the Claude Platform, Amazon Bedrock, Claude Platform on AWS, Google’s Agent Platform, and Microsoft Foundry, with equivalent controls on AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure. Anthropic doesn’t charge for EFS; the customer’s cloud provider bills the storage, reads, writes, and egress like any other resource.

Who shaped it

A standard built with regulated industries

Anthropic developed EFS with more than 100 customers across financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, telecom, law, retail, and the public sector, and with cloud partners AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure. One group was the Analysis and Resilience Center for Systemic Risk (ARC), whose members include the CISOs of the largest US banks — Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Citi, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo. Leaders at Comcast, KPMG, Mastercard, Salesforce, Visa, Snowflake, and Stripe also weighed in.

Banking

“Who holds the keys?”

ARC members defined what it takes to run frontier models inside a systemically important bank: who holds the data, who holds the keys, what automated review can see, and when a human may ever look.

Platforms

Architecture, not just policy

Snowflake and Stripe framed the win as structural: data stays in the customer’s environment under keys they control, so the strictest data guarantees hold while the most capable models run from day one.

What to do about it

EFS rolls out in phases toward broad availability later this fall, and eligible customers get zero data retention on Fable 5 and 5.1 until it’s ready. If retention has been your blocker, request access and start scoping which cloud account would hold the monitoring data.
