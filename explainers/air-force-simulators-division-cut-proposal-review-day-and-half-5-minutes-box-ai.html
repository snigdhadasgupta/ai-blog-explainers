1.5 Days to 5 Minutes

The U.S. Air Force Simulators Division replaced a broken file-sharing system with Box, opened secure access to hundreds of contractors, then layered Box AI agents on top — compressing initial proposal review from a day and a half to about five minutes.

The idea in five lines

  • From a broken system to open access — the Division swapped a legacy site capped at two CAC-holding users per company for Box, now serving 350 external industry partners.
  • Review time collapsed — checking proposals from seven or eight companies once took 20 people a day to a day and a half; Box AI does the initial pass in about five minutes.
  • Build the agent once, swap the docs — an agent compares each response against the technical document and evaluation criteria, then runs per company with a single change.

So what for you

The pattern travels: fix the boring content and access foundation first, then layer AI on top for the slowest step — and keep AI as a cited first-pass reviewer, not the decision-maker, especially where accountability is non-negotiable.

The bottleneck

Two users per company, and lockouts

The Simulators Division builds the training systems pilots and maintenance crews rely on, coordinating a Training Systems Acquisition contract that now involves 36 industry partners. For years, sharing information meant a legacy site with a hard ceiling: two people per company, each needing a Common Access Card. Contractors got locked out when IT changed rules remotely, forcing fallbacks to blast emails, paper copies, and CDs.

“Coordinating proposal reviews with industry partners used to be a logistical bottleneck.” — Joe Erickson Jr., Program Manager, Simulators Division
A friction-free foundation

From 2 users to 350 partners

The Division adopted Box as its primary platform for external collaboration — secure file sharing plus a structured file-request channel for contractor proposals. Where the old system allowed two CAC-holding users per company, Box opened access to everyone who needed it: today the Division runs 30 internal licenses and 350 external users, with granular access controls and no government credentials required. The file-request link became a standard part of the procurement process.

AI supports, not decides

Human judgment stays in the loop

The most important part of how the Division uses Box AI is what it does not do: it does not make decisions. AI provides a high-level initial analysis — helping the team spot whether a proposal meets requirements and where to look closer — but the source-selection decision is a person’s. That distinction matters in a defense environment where trust and accountability are non-negotiable, and it is reinforced by running only FedRAMP High and IL-4 authorized AI models.

A model for other agencies

Fix the broken collaboration and access foundation first, give the office a single auditable place for submissions, then layer AI on the most time-consuming step — freeing the team to spend more time supporting the mission and the warfighter.
