Microsoft Copilot for Legal

Microsoft is bringing legal-specific AI into Word and Cowork: more than 20 purpose-built skills built alongside legal engineers, plus partner plugins for research, matter management, e-signature, and e-discovery. Copilot proposes redlines as native tracked changes and applies them only on approval — keeping the lawyer's judgment on the decisions only they can make.

The idea in five lines

  • 20+ purpose-built legal skills are generally available, built with legal engineers to translate real legal standards into Word and Cowork workflows.
  • Redlines as native tracked changes — Copilot decides how a clause should change; Word applies it as a tracked edit and preserves formatting, approved only by the user.
  • Work spans portfolios, not one doc — Cowork tracks obligations and deadlines across contract portfolios, compares terms, and builds case chronologies.
  • Connected to the legal stack — partner plugins reach Bloomberg Law, iManage, NetDocuments, Litera, Harvey, Relativity, Docusign and more via the plugin registry.
  • Measured on real edits — since the April Frontier release, 1,800+ users generated nearly 100,000 edits and kept roughly seven in ten.
So what for you

If your legal team lives in Word, pilot the redlining and playbook-review skills first — the tracked-change, approve-before-apply model fits how lawyers already work, and the keep-rate on edits gives you a concrete signal to watch. Scope partner plugins to the two or three research and document systems your team actually uses.

The problem

The answer rarely sits in one place

Legal work depends on precedent, organizational standards, and regulatory considerations that can change the whole analysis, so lawyers need to understand what an answer rests on before applying judgment. These are not prompts layered on a general model: each skill combines legal-specific reasoning with native Microsoft 365 behavior. When redlining, Copilot determines how a clause should change while Word applies the edit as a tracked change and preserves formatting; when drafting, it pulls approved precedents from SharePoint and uses Word content controls for structured, editable placeholders. Work IQ connects it to the matters, standards, and context behind the document.

I don't have to change how I work — something that might take an hour to review initially can often take several minutes, so I get to the substantive issues faster.

The surface & the numbers

Connected systems, and what early use shows

New partner-built plugins bring specialized sources into Copilot, rolling out through the Microsoft plugin registry: legal research (Bloomberg Law, Trellis, Descrybe, Legal Data Hunter, Courtroom5, Lawstronaut); document, matter, and transaction management (iManage Work, NetDocuments, Box, Litera, HighQ from Thomson Reuters, LegalZoom, Solve Intelligence); agreements and e-signature (Docusign, Juro, Adobe Acrobat Sign, LawVu, SignatureAPI, Dropbox Sign, PandaDoc); specialized legal AI (Harvey, Legora, DeepJudge); and e-discovery (Everlaw, Relativity).

Governance mirrors the rest of Copilot: proposed edits are reviewed and applied only with the user's approval; processing stays within the Microsoft 365 service boundary with enterprise data protection; Copilot works only with content each user can access, honors sensitivity labels, and does not train foundation models on prompts, responses, or documents.

Where to start

Turn on the Word skills for a drafting-and-negotiation team, keep them set to review-before-apply (the default), and watch the keep-rate on suggested edits as your signal. Add the one or two research and document-management plugins your matters depend on, not the whole registry.
