Pro-level AI images, native to Workspace

AI image generation took off for personal use, but business teams got inconsistent results, endless trial-and-error prompting, and clunky cross-app workflows. Google Pics puts generation and precise, object-level editing straight into the apps teams already work in — starting with Docs and Slides.

The idea in five lines

  • A native imaging app — Google Pics is now rolling out inside Workspace for AI image generation and editing, reached at pics.new.
  • Built for the business gap — where consumer tools left teams fighting inconsistent output and multi-app workflows for marketing, decks, and product visuals.
  • Precision, not just prompting — select and edit individual objects, reformat or translate text elements, crop, and upscale to 2K or 4K.
  • Edits where you work — select an image in Docs or Slides and refine it with the full Pics toolset in one click, no app-switching.
  • Rolling out on paid tiers — Business and Enterprise Standard/Plus, plus Google AI Pro, Ultra, and AI Pro for Education plans.
So what for you

If your team already pays for a Business or Enterprise Workspace plan, Pics is likely included in what you have — try replacing one recurring design-request loop (a deck graphic, a social asset, a flyer) with an in-app edit before you commission or buy another tool for it.

The problem it targets

Consumer image AI didn’t translate to business work

AI image generation became a hit for personal use, but the enterprise story lagged. Teams trying to produce professional-grade visuals for marketing campaigns, customer presentations, and product storytelling ran into inconsistent results, endless trial-and-error prompting, and cumbersome workflows stitched across multiple apps. The output was hard to control and harder to finish.

Pics eliminates the frustration of wrangling pixels and feeling like you need to be a professional designer to deliver a great image.

How it works

From a prompt to a finished, on-format asset

You start at pics.new by importing an image from your computer, Drive, or Google Photos — or by typing a prompt to generate one, with a set of alternatives offered so you are not stuck refining a single guess. The real shift is what comes next: precise, object-based editing tools that take a rough generation to a usable asset without a designer in the loop.

The editing toolset

What “precision editing” actually means here

Generation gets an image close; these controls are what make it finishable without a design tool. Each acts on parts of an image rather than forcing a full regenerate.

Objects

Select and edit in place

Target individual objects within an image and change them directly, instead of re-prompting the whole scene and hoping the rest survives.

Text

Edit, reformat, translate

Work on individual text elements — including translating them — so a single asset can be localized for global markets without a rebuild.

Format

Crop, upscale, batch

Crop to web, social, print, or digital dimensions, upscale to 2K or 4K, and process multiple edits at once, with easy revert on anything you do not want.

Where it lives

In the apps, not in another tab

Because Pics is part of Workspace, you can edit imagery inside the tools you already use: select an image in Google Docs or Slides and a single click opens the full Pics toolset without switching apps or tabs. It carries the collaboration model of Docs, Sheets, and Drive — invite teammates, share by link, and create or open Pics images in Drive. Google says that in the coming weeks you will also be able to open and edit nearly any image saved in Drive with one click.

Reach

Every team, most projects

Intended for teams to produce social, digital, and web assets for global markets, place product concepts into any background, and brainstorm print collateral, flyers, business cards, and branded merchandise.

Access

Paid Workspace & AI plans

Rolling out on Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, Google AI Pro and Ultra personal plans, and Google AI Pro for Education — usable today at pics.new.

What to do with it

Pick one visual task your team currently outsources or avoids — a recurring deck graphic, a localized social asset, an event flyer — and run it through Pics inline in Docs or Slides. If the object-level edits hold up, fold it into that workflow before renewing or buying a separate design tool for the same job.
