Compliance built into the content, not around it

Life sciences lives by one rule: move fast, but don’t cut a single regulatory corner. Tissium — a startup out of MIT reconstructing human tissue with programmable polymers — put Box underneath its research, operations, and AI so that speed never comes at the cost of governance.

The idea in five lines

  • Content is the control point — Tissium runs collaboration, workflows, and AI on Box as one governed content layer, so governance isn’t bolted on after the fact.
  • Sprawl was the real risk — folders had multiplied until no one could tell which file was current; a structured migration cut over “overnight” and put everything in its right place.
  • Compliance engineered in — GxP, GDPR, EU MDR, and US 21 CFR Part 11 readiness are supported at the platform layer, with computer-system validation streamlined under the GAMP 5 framework.
  • Batch records, 15 min to 2 — a Box Automate proof of concept digitized handwritten batch records, cutting roughly 15 minutes per batch to about 2 and running in weeks, not a multi-year rollout.
  • Ten years, put to work — archived R&D data is segregated into a permissioned Hub, so a decade of clinical knowledge stays searchable without exposing the company’s IP.
So what for you

If you operate in a regulated industry, fix your content structure before you add AI — classification, archiving, and least-privilege permissions first. As Tissium’s team puts it, your output is only as good as your input, so cleaning the input is the fastest path to AI you can actually put in front of an auditor.

Why it matters

Trust is the prerequisite, not a feature

Tissium came out of MIT labs, where a polymer showed promise for human tissue reconstruction. The company now develops biomorphic, programmable polymers that repair damaged tissue and replace stitches and other penetrating techniques during surgery. In that world, the team is ultimately accountable for people’s lives — so every platform it adopts has to be vetted before it is trusted. By centralizing research and operational content on Box as a governed content layer, Tissium’s teams can lean on AI and automation without loosening their grip on governance.

“Trust, for us, is a prerequisite… every platform we use has to be vetted.” — Franck-Aurèle Medegan, Director, ESG & Finance, Tissium

The starting point

First they fixed the sprawl

Before Box, content sat in a traditional folder hierarchy that had been allowed to grow unchecked — hard to search, hard to permission, and, in the team’s own words, frankly a mess. They knew they couldn’t move forward until the data was structured properly. Box Consulting met teams across the organization to learn how they actually used content, what access each group needed, and what was shared externally — then migrated everything in a single overnight cutover.

“It was a long night, but in the morning, everything was in the right place.”

The headline result

A three-year project, delivered in weeks

Batch records — the compliance paperwork for every production run — were handwritten, often spanning up to 20 separate files of 5 to 15 pages each. The standard fix, a full electronic batch record (EBR) system, would have meant roughly three years of implementation plus complex FDA validation. Instead, Tissium ran a Box Automate proof of concept that digitized the flow from the initial production decision through clean-room printing. The hard part was designing what the process should actually be; once that was clear, implementation was fast.

How it is built

Structure first: classify, archive, least privilege

The migration wasn’t a lift-and-shift — it encoded a permissions architecture designed to keep intellectual property, the company’s most valuable asset, protected by default.

Classify

Automated classification

Box classification assesses and tags confidential files automatically, so IP protection travels with the content instead of depending on where someone filed it.

Archive

Segregated R&D Hub

Older data moves to dedicated archive folders and a permissioned Box Hub — active queries stay clean while a decade of clinical knowledge remains searchable.

Share

Controlled external access

Externally shareable content sits in its own folder with specific permissions, with Box Hubs as the curated destination for approved external sharing.

“Your output is only as good as your input. Spending time cleaning the input is the most effective way to get reliable output.”

Compliance + AI

Audit-ready by design — and AI kept on a short leash

As a governed content layer, Box supports EU-regulated life sciences with built-in GDPR and GxP controls aligned to EU MDR, plus 21 CFR Part 11 readiness for the US market, where the FDA asks different questions than European notified bodies. A dedicated compliance portal and documentation streamlined computer-system validation under the GAMP 5 framework — the difference between automation that slows an audit and automation that survives one.

On AI, Tissium is deliberately conservative: it is experimenting with permissions-aware access through the Box MCP server and workflow integrations via Box APIs to query ten years of structured and unstructured R&D data. But AI is strictly an assistant — easing tasks like translation and study retrieval — not an autonomous decision-maker.

“Now, we can build automation securely knowing that if we are audited, it won’t be a problem.”

What this means for regulated teams

You don’t have to choose between moving fast and staying compliant — but the order matters. Consolidate onto one governed platform, encode classification and least-privilege permissions in the structure itself, then layer AI and automation on top. That sequence is what let a startup trade a three-year EBR project for a weeks-long proof of concept without taking on audit risk.
