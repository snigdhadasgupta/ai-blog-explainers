Passing the efficiency gains on to the bill

A day after showing how GPT-5.6 made itself cheaper to run, OpenAI hands those savings to customers: Luna drops 80%, Terra 20%, and a new Fast mode lets Sol run 2.5× faster when speed is worth the premium.

The idea in five lines

  • Prices come down — GPT-5.6 Luna now costs 80% less and Terra 20% less, in the API and against paid Codex and ChatGPT Work quotas.
  • New Fast mode for Sol — up to 2.5× faster than Standard at twice the price, with no change in intelligence; it replaces Priority Processing.
  • Match intelligence to the outcome — pick a tier per step of a workflow rather than defaulting to the flagship for everything.
  • Cheap enough to broaden use — Luna delivers year-ago frontier quality at roughly six cents on the dollar per task, and nearly 9× the speed.
  • Efficiency, not discounting — the cuts reflect real gains across models, inference, and the agentic harness, not a promotional price.
So what for you

Before you renew a flagship-only AI budget, re-run your evaluations against the cheaper tiers — high-volume steps like classification, extraction, and routine implementation may now clear your quality bar at a fraction of the cost, freeing budget for the few tasks that truly need the top model.

What changed

The savings move from the lab to the invoice

OpenAI frames this as the sequel to its engineering post on GPT-5.6 efficiency: yesterday it explained how the model made itself cheaper to run, and today it passes those gains to customers. Lower prices land for the two workhorse tiers — Luna, the fastest and most affordable model, and Terra, the balanced everyday model — while Sol gains a speed option. The same reductions flow through to how usage counts against paid Codex and ChatGPT Work subscriptions, so budgets stretch further without a plan change.

Where the savings come from

Three layers, optimized together

The price cuts are downstream of efficiency work across the whole stack, not a margin decision. GPT-5.6 models take a more direct path through a task; better routing keeps hardware productive; optimized serving software generates tokens more cheaply; and smarter context management stops agents repeating completed work.

Compute strategy

Matching each workload to the right system

Meeting demand for abundant intelligence takes both more compute and more productive compute. OpenAI describes a resilient infrastructure portfolio that routes each workload to the systems best suited to run it, supporting both ends of the price-performance curve at once.

Low-cost end

High volume becomes economical

New Luna and Terra prices make large-scale document analysis, customer-interaction classification, and routine implementation practical to run broadly — work that was previously too costly to automate at scale.

Frontier end

Fast mode when speed matters

Fast mode gives API customers quicker access to Sol on the most consequential work, so complex jobs can move faster when the premium is justified — without changing the model’s intelligence.

What to do about it

Audit which workflows still run on your most expensive model out of habit. Move high-volume, well-specified steps to Luna or Terra, keep Sol (optionally in Fast mode) for the hard calls, and let evaluations — not defaults — decide where extra intelligence actually pays for itself.
