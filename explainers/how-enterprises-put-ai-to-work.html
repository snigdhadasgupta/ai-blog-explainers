From Assistance to Execution

OpenAI’s two new studies show enterprise AI shifting from asking to doing — and a small group of “frontier” firms pulling sharply ahead by connecting agents to their real context, tools, and repeatable workflows.

The idea in five lines

  • From asking to doing — enterprise AI is shifting from assistants that help people think to agents that complete the work.
  • The frontier gap is widening — top-10% firms now generate 8.3× the output tokens per user of typical firms, up from 2.6× in January.
  • Work is going agentic — Codex generated 64% of combined Codex and ChatGPT enterprise output tokens as of June.
  • Advanced capability is the divider — 21% of frontier-firm users touch Plugins weekly versus 9% at typical firms; skills 19% versus 3%.
  • Agents spread beyond engineering — since February, weekly Codex users grew 108× in legal, 41× in sales and recruiting, and 26× in marketing.
So what for you

The gap isn’t about model access — everyone has the same models. To close it, find the individual workflows already working in your team, connect agents to your real context and tools with clear permissions and human review, and turn those wins into shared, repeatable practice.

The shift

Assistants help you think; agents help you finish

Enterprise AI is moving from answering questions to carrying out work. Products like ChatGPT Work and Codex can use tools, create files, and produce work for review. Instead of asking how to prepare a presentation, a worker can ask an agent to gather information across sources and draft the deck itself.

The shift is visible in the numbers: as of June, Codex generated 64% of combined Codex and ChatGPT output tokens among enterprise customers. Agentic workflows produce more output because they run longer, multi-step tasks — so the figure reflects both how often Codex is used and how substantial the work is.

By connecting agents to company context and tools — with clear permissions, governance, and human review — firms move from assistance to execution.

The gap appears across industries and company sizes, not just tech firms. And among the U.S. public companies studied, enterprise adopters showed stronger financials than non-adopters — more assets, more workers, higher R&D — suggesting access alone isn’t what scales AI.

What frontier firms do differently

Context and tools, not just a better model

Agents need the right context and tools to be effective. Plugins bundle capabilities for a specific workflow — combining skills (reusable instructions) with apps that connect to company data, tools, and actions. A sales Plugin, for example, can pair a team’s playbook with its CRM so an agent drafts a tailored response for review using current customer information.

Connect

Wire agents to real context

Give agents access to the systems where the work lives — CRM, docs, tickets — so they act on current data, not a static prompt.

Govern

Permissions and review

Establish clear permissions, human review, and governance before agents take consequential actions — the guardrails that make delegation safe.

Standardize

Turn wins into workflows

Convert effective individual use into shared, repeatable practices so a good workflow becomes a team capability, not a one-off.

Even frontier-firm adoption is a fraction of what’s possible: at OpenAI, 95% of employees use Plugins weekly — a marker of how much headroom remains.

Who’s using it

The adoption curve runs from the bottom up

Surprising

Early-career, not executives

Despite surveys claiming leaders lead adoption, conversation-level data finds the opposite: six months after adoption, early-career employees sent 13 more messages per week than executives — a possible comparative advantage worth making visible.

Proof point

Virgin Atlantic, across the business

Engineering teams use Codex to refactor legacy code in 30 minutes instead of two weeks; product teams use ChatGPT Work to compress weeks of competitive research into hours, shaping a five-year digital strategy.

What leaders can do to close the gap

Extend agentic workflows beyond engineering, identify the employees with the strongest AI habits and make their workflows visible, and turn successful individual use cases into repeatable practice — connecting agents to context and tools with clear permissions, governance, and human review.
