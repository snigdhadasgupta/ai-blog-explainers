Frontier intelligence, at frontier efficiency

OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 family is engineered for intelligence-per-dollar: a tiered lineup (Sol, Terra, Luna) plus deep optimizations across inference and the agentic harness — many of them landed by GPT-5.6 Sol itself running inside Codex.

The idea in five lines

  • Efficiency is the headline — GPT-5.6 is trained to do more work per token, pushing useful intelligence per dollar rather than raw capability alone.
  • A tiered family — Sol (flagship), Terra (mid), and Luna (fastest, cheapest) let teams pick a point on the cost-intelligence curve.
  • Gains across the stack — improvements span the models, the inference layer, and the agentic harness that Codex and ChatGPT Work run on.
  • The model tuned itself — GPT-5.6 Sol in Codex rewrote production kernels, improved its own speculative-decoding draft model, and tuned serving configs.
  • It compounds — kernel work cut serving cost ~20%, speculative decoding lifted token generation 15%+, and load balancing dropped serving cost further.
So what for you

When you pick a model for a workflow, match the tier to the task instead of defaulting to the flagship — a cheaper tier often clears the bar at a fraction of the cost. The same “more work per token” logic applies to your own agents: trimming context and reusing cached prompts is where real spend is saved.

Why efficiency, not just capability

Intelligence per dollar is the new frontier

With demand growing faster than compute capacity, OpenAI frames efficiency as central to distributing AI broadly — now serving 1 billion active users and more than 2 million businesses. GPT-5.6 is trained to achieve more work per token, optimizing for both task success and a direct path through a task. The result is a family designed to hit the best performance at every point on the cost-intelligence curve, not a single expensive flagship.

Any isolated improvement may seem limited, but these wins compound to deliver on the frontier of both intelligence and efficiency.

The agentic harness

Cutting repeated work inside every turn

A single user turn in Codex or ChatGPT Work can fan out into dozens of model requests and tool calls. Any cost inside that repeated region is paid many times, so the harness — a Rust orchestration layer — is designed to remove overhead per request.

Context

Avoid context bloat

Deferred discovery surfaces integrations, MCP tools, skills, and plugins only when needed. Tool output is capped at 10,000 tokens by default unless the model asks for more — keeping cost and distraction down.

Caching

Preserve exact prefixes

Model-visible history is append-only and tools are presented in deterministic order, so prompt-cache prefixes stay intact. That drives high cache-hit rates across Codex and ChatGPT Work, avoiding recomputation of repeated inputs.

What to do about it

Right-size the model to the task, lean on cheaper tiers where they clear the bar, and apply the same discipline to your own agents: cap tool output, defer tool discovery, and keep prompt prefixes stable so caching pays off. Efficiency is a design choice, not just a model spec.
