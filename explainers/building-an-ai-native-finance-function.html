Building an AI-Native Finance Function

OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar’s five lessons for redesigning finance around AI — from a zero-day close and continuous forecasting to clear controls and an ROI scorecard measured per unit of intelligence.

So what for you

Pick one consequential, recurring decision — the month-end close or a forecast review — and map its full data-to-sign-off path before automating anything. That reframing, more than any single tool, is where the ROI comes from.

From static reports to live decisions

Finance as a real-time function

Closing the books and updating forecasts still involves manual, recurring work: finding information, explaining what changed, and assembling the inputs for a decision. OpenAI set two ambitions to break that pattern — a zero-day close that gives leaders a real-time, reconciled, traceable view of the company’s position, and continuous forecasting that shows how the business is changing and which decisions could alter the outcome.

Neither is finished, but the work has already pushed the team past static spreadsheets and manual record-hunting toward live tools built on the full context of the business — and given finance professionals the ability to build the tools their own work requires.

The real promise: a team that understands what’s happening as it happens, shows leaders the choices ahead, and gives the business more time to act while the outcome can still change.

The five lessons

A practical pattern any CFO can apply

Lesson 1

Access + a reason to use it

Put secure, capable AI in people’s hands, then pair it with structured experimentation on real problems — a finance hackathon produced IR-GPT, grounded in approved investor materials.

Lesson 2

Redesign around the decision

Map the data, tools, approvals, and handoffs a decision needs, then decide which parts AI can analyze, coordinate, or complete — improving the whole cycle, not one task.

Lesson 3

Finance pros become builders

Teammates build live dashboards with ChatGPT Work and Codex. One who’d never coded built a tool turning a monthly ad forecast into weekly and daily plans, every number tied to the approved model.

Lesson 4

Speed with accountability

Define what data AI can access, what it can do, when approval is required, and when to escalate. Every output ties to a source; every baseline change needs finance authorization.

Lesson 5

Measure per unit of intelligence

Seats and tokens don’t tell you much. Judge each workflow on whether the work got done well and what it truly cost — the cheapest model isn’t always the most economical.

Controls and the ROI scorecard

How to keep speed accountable

The “tokenmaxxing” craze has come and gone; it’s now straightforward to set usage limits, budget controls, role-based access, model-routing rules, and approval thresholds — and manage AI with the same discipline as any variable expense. For each workflow, the team asks four questions:

Figure 1 · The value-per-unit-of-intelligence scorecard
1

Did it matter?

Did AI complete work that actually mattered — a forecast updated, a variance explained, a board question answered?

2

What did it cost?

Including employee time, review, and rework — not just tokens.

3

Good enough?

Was the result reliable enough to use without heavy correction?

4

Faster or better?

Did it help the team move faster or make a better decision?

↻ For the close, track cycle time, auto-reconciled share, exceptions, and time to explain a variance.

The human role stays central: AI accelerates synthesis, reconciliation, and preparation; finance validates the numbers, applies judgment, and owns the sign-off.

The CFO’s opportunity

Finance sits at the center of strategy, capital, data, and risk — a strong vantage point to lead an AI transformation. Begin with a meaningful workflow, expand through evidence, keep accountability clear, and measure the outcome.
