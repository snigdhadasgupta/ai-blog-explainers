The CISO’s Two Jobs in the Agentic Era

Agents have spread across enterprises faster than anyone planned. Microsoft’s security leadership argues the CISO now holds two jobs at once: put guardrails around the agents your own people are building, and defend differently against attackers moving at AI speed.

The idea in five lines

  • Agent sprawl is here — active agents in the Microsoft 365 ecosystem grew 15x year over year, and 18x in large enterprises.
  • Non-engineers are building them — employees are creating agents that access company data and act on their behalf, often without thinking about security the way a security team would.
  • Job one: guardrails — deep observability over which agents exist and what they can reach, plus a governed identity and classified data for every agent.
  • Job two: defend differently — AI-driven attackers compress response time, so integrate signals across identity, endpoints, apps, and cloud, and put agents alongside analysts.
  • Not a slowdown — the goal is to let the business adopt AI safely, not to stop it.
So what for you

If anyone on your team is building an agent, have the identity-and-data conversation with security before you build, not after — give the agent its own governed identity and only the access it needs. A broad credential granted “just to ship it” is the risk that compounds.

Why it matters

The enterprise now runs on agents

Agents have spread faster than most organizations anticipated, and the people building them increasingly aren’t engineers. A well-intentioned agent still adds risk when its access and behavior aren’t governed — it will act on any access it has, and it doesn’t share a security team’s judgment. That reality reshapes the CISO role into two simultaneous shifts: protect an enterprise that increasingly operates through AI, and rethink defense as attackers use AI to move faster and at greater scale.

Give agents their own governed identities, protect the data they can touch, and have the security conversation early — before building.

The two jobs

Guardrails, and a new way to defend

Job 1 · Protect

Put guardrails around agents

Deep observability: know which agents exist, who owns them, what they can reach, what they’re doing, and when behavior changes. Hold firm on identity and data — every agent gets a governed identity with only the access it needs, over data that’s been classified and protected.

Job 2 · Defend

Defend differently

AI-driven attackers compress the defender’s response window. The shift is in how teams turn information into action — integrating signals across identity, endpoints, applications, and cloud with threat intelligence, and putting agents to work alongside analysts to investigate and respond.

For the people building

Three rules before an agent ships

Figure 1 · What builders owe security
1

Give it an identity

Assign a governed identity rather than personal or shared credentials, so you can tell which agent did what — and shut it off.

2

Protect the data

An agent acts on any access it has and doesn’t share your judgment, so constrain and classify what it can reach.

3

Talk to security early

Have the identity-and-data conversation before building, not after an incident.

4

Watch for drift

Observe behavior over time and flag when an agent starts doing something new.

↻ Scale: active agents grew 15x year over year across Microsoft 365, and 18x in large enterprises.

The bottom line

Enable AI without unmanaged risk: governed identity and classified data for every agent, observability over what agents do, security involved early — and a defense that integrates signals and uses agents to keep pace with AI-enabled threats.
