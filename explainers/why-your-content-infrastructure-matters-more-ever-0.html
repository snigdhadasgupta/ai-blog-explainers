Content Is the Agent’s Workspace

As organizations race to adopt agents and autonomous workflows, the ones scaling AI successfully focus on something less glamorous than the latest model: secure, governed content. Box argues that content is where agents think, store their work, and communicate — so the platform under it has to be built for agents, not just people.

The idea in five lines

  • Agents are digital workers — they reason, plan, run code, call APIs, coordinate, and keep memory across sessions, and like human workers they need a governed platform to succeed.
  • Context lives in your content — models are only as useful as the context they can reach, and your intellectual capital sits in unstructured PDFs, docs, decks, and images.
  • Every output stays governed — agent results are automatically saved, versioned, and cited to source, inheriting the same permissions, retention, and audit controls as a human’s view.
  • A multi-agent world needs one content layer — IDC projects 70% of top AI-driven enterprises will run multi-tool architectures by 2028, each model needing consistent, permission-aware access to the same content.
  • Scale makes governance non-negotiable — soon hundreds of thousands of agents will access content at machine speed, so access controls, audit logs, and lineage must apply to agents exactly as they do to people.
So what for you

Before expanding agent pilots, ask four questions of your content: can agents authenticate and act on it via API, can you govern their access at human-grade rigor, can you audit every action back to source, and can the infrastructure handle the volume. If the honest answer is “not yet,” that’s the work to prioritize.

The shift

Platforms built for people meet a workforce of agents

For most of computing history, platforms assumed human users navigating folders and deciding where to save work. Today’s most capable AI systems are autonomous — they reason, plan, execute, run code, call APIs, coordinate with other agents, and hold persistent memory. They’re effectively digital workers, and the platform they work on is still built on content: when an agent researches, it stores content; when it generates a report, the output is content; when it collaborates, it shares content.

A content platform is the workspace where agents think, the repository where they store their work, and the medium through which they communicate.

A multi-agent world

The universal content layer

Enterprises won’t choose between Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Cursor, and custom agents — they’ll run several at once, each needing access to the same organizational content with its own integration patterns. IDC projects that by 2028, 70% of top AI-driven enterprises will use advanced multi-tool architectures. Box’s pitch is to be the single source of content truth, reachable through standardized interfaces that work for people, applications, and agents alike.

Why governance gets harder

The same controls, applied to agents

When humans access sensitive information, organizations lean on training, policy, and trust. Those safeguards don’t automatically transfer to an agent — and the speed and scale of autonomous work make governance more important, not less. Box applies granular access controls to agents as to humans, logs every agent action, applies classification and retention to agent-generated content automatically, and tracks content lineage to show which agent created or modified any file.
