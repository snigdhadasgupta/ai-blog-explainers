Box Puts GPT-6 Astra to Work

Box benchmarks frontier models the way its customers actually use them — complex, multi-document business tasks run through an agent. On that eval, OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra reached 77% overall accuracy, ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol’s 74% — and pulled far further ahead on the hardest workflows.

The idea in five lines

  • Box benchmarks real work — it scores models on complex, multi-document business tasks run through an agent harness, not trivia questions.
  • Astra hits the frontier — GPT-6 Astra scored 77% overall accuracy against GPT-5.6 Sol’s 74% on the same eval.
  • The gap widens on hard tasks — on the most demanding workflows the jump is large, e.g. legal review 69% to 93% and energy reporting 82% to 97%.
  • It knows what it doesn’t know — Astra labels a derived figure a proxy, cites the governing clause, and flags a bad reading as a data-quality issue rather than missing data.
  • Decision-ready, not just plausible — the payoff is less time checking the model and less risk of an early error cascading into a high-stakes call.
So what for you

When you’re choosing a model for document-heavy work, weight how it handles ambiguity and sourcing over the headline benchmark average. A three-point overall gap hides much bigger differences on exactly the tasks — contracts, reconciliations, audits — where a confident wrong answer costs the most.

Why this benchmark is different

Graded on the work, not the quiz

Box evaluates models on tasks that mirror the workflows its customers run every day. Each one runs on Box’s agent harness: the model has to find the right documents, reason across spreadsheets, PDFs, presentations and images, and produce a finished deliverable a knowledge worker could hand off — a cost analysis, a diligence findings list, a verification audit.

On that eval, GPT-6 Astra reached 77% overall accuracy, ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol’s 74%. The headline gap is modest — but the averages flatten a much bigger story hiding in the hardest tasks.

The difference isn’t an output that looks plausible; it’s a deliverable a team can confidently act on.

Where Astra pulls ahead

Four hard tasks, four bigger gaps

Astra’s strength showed up where documents disagree with each other, where the deliverable needs a figure nobody handed you, and where quantitative data has to be reconciled with narrative, contractual or domain-specific source material. Task accuracy, GPT-5.6 Sol → GPT-6 Astra:

Figure 1 · Per-task accuracy on Box's complex-work eval
Media & entertainment48% → 100%
Ranking film genres and countries by profitability while applying the next year’s tax-incentive corrections without double-counting. Astra was perfect every attempt; Sol had the rankings right but the ratios wrong.
Technology69% → 97%
Choosing which region to fund from documents that never state the metric the brief asks for. Astra flagged the gap, labelled its own figure a proxy, and caught a growth claim that didn’t match the numbers. Sol reported the proxy as the real thing.
Legal69% → 93%
Reviewing an NDA against a contracting policy, where the verdict must cite the provision behind it. Only Astra separated whether the liability cap’s structure was permissible from whether its amount was defensible, and pointed to the settling language.
Energy82% → 97%
Building a consumption report where only one of two sites is actually missing data. Astra drew the line most reviewers wouldn’t: the other site’s odd solar readings are a measurement problem to investigate, not a gap. Sol called both incomplete.

The pattern underneath is consistent: Astra distinguishes a verified metric from a proxy, traces a conclusion to the governing source, and separates a data-quality anomaly from genuinely missing information — the judgment calls that decide whether an automated deliverable is trustworthy.
