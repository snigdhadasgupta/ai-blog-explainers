What to Look For in AI Enterprise Search

Most employees still can’t reliably find the file they need — and bolting a chatbot onto a broken index doesn’t fix it. Box lays out what actually separates AI enterprise search from keyword search, and the five capabilities to insist on before you buy.

The idea in five lines

  • Keyword search keeps failing — scattered files, inconsistent tags, and mixed permissions mean most people still can’t find what they need.
  • The shift is intent plus permissions — AI search reads meaning, not just keywords, and only ever returns what each user is allowed to see.
  • Grounded answers with citations — it connects approved repositories, builds semantic indexes, and answers from source documents you can verify.
  • Five things to demand — semantic retrieval, agentic refinement, permission-aware relevance, metadata-driven accuracy, and unified access across silos.
  • Treat it as a rollout — prepare and map data, pilot, align governance, then expand — and measure search success, time saved, and stale-file errors.
So what for you

If you’re evaluating an enterprise search or “ask your company” tool, make permission-aware retrieval and grounded, cited answers non-negotiable. A fast answer that surfaces restricted files or cites nothing is worse than no answer — it moves fast and quietly erodes trust.

The problem

The index is broken, not the search box

Company knowledge is spread across tools, tagged inconsistently, and layered with permissions. Classic keyword and metadata search assumes clean, well-labeled content — so when content is fragmented, it quietly fails. Box frames AI search that understands intent and permissions as the fix, and anchors the problem with three third-party figures it cites.

The checklist

Five capabilities to demand

Strip the marketing away and Box’s most useful contribution is a buying checklist. These are the capabilities that separate real AI enterprise search from a search box with a language model stapled on.

01

Semantic, intent-driven

Retrieval on meaning, not literal keyword matches — so a query works even when it doesn’t use the document’s exact words.

02

Agentic refinement

Asks clarifying questions and supports conversational follow-ups instead of returning a flat list and leaving you to re-query.

03

Permission-aware relevance

Honors each user’s access rights so results never surface content the person isn’t authorized to see.

04

Metadata-driven accuracy

Uses version history and file attributes to return the latest approved version, not a stale or superseded copy.

05

Unified across silos

Reaches connected repositories as one corpus rather than forcing separate searches per system.

Plus

Grounded, cited answers

Every answer points back to the source document so employees can verify it — the difference between a confident guess and a defensible result.

How it works

From repositories to grounded answers

Under the hood, Box describes a secure, permission-aware search layer built on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): connect approved sources, extract text and metadata, build semantic indexes, enforce role-based permissions, then generate answers grounded in the retrieved documents.

Figure 2 · The permission-aware RAG pipeline
1

Connect sources

Index approved repositories across the systems where content already lives.

2

Extract & enrich

Pull text and metadata so meaning and file attributes are both searchable.

3

Permission filter

Enforce role-based access so retrieval only ever considers what the user may see.

4

Ground & cite

Generate the answer from retrieved docs and link back to the source to verify.

Rollout & measurement

Four phases, and what to measure

1

Prepare & map data

Inventory sources, clean content, and map where knowledge actually lives.

2

Pilot

Tune and test on a bounded corpus and a real user group before scaling.

3

Align governance

Lock in labeling, retention, permissions, and audit before broad access.

4

Expand

Extend to more repositories and teams once quality and controls hold.

Box’s suggested metrics are the ones worth lifting: search success rate, time saved, reduction in duplicate questions, fewer errors from outdated files, user satisfaction, and faster onboarding. On governance, it stresses permission-aware retrieval, role-based access, encryption, audit logs, retention, classification, and the ability to explain why a result appeared — plus human review where it matters.

What to do with this

Use the five-capability checklist as your RFP. Ask every vendor to demo permission-aware results on your messy content, show a grounded answer with a working citation, and explain why a given result surfaced. Then run a bounded pilot and track search success and time saved before you expand.
