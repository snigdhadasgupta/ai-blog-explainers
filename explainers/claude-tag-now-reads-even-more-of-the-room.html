Claude Tag now reads the whole room

Claude’s Slack teammate used to judge every message on its own. Now it reads context from across the channel — plus its memory and your standing instructions — to decide when to jump in, and when to stay out of the way.

The idea in five lines

  • Reads the channel now — Claude Tag uses context from across a Slack channel, not one message at a time, plus its memory and your standing instructions.
  • Sharper judgment — it is roughly 30% better at deciding when, and when not, to respond without being asked.
  • Four moves — reply inline, open a thread for deeper work, route a message into work already in flight, or say nothing.
  • Knows when to go quiet — in a channel where it keeps having nothing to add, it goes to sleep; an @-mention wakes it instantly.
  • No extra cost — the added context does not count toward usage or spend limits; live today for Teams and Enterprise.
So what for you

If your team already runs Claude Tag, revisit which channels have “respond automatically” on. The smarter proactivity pays off most in a few high-signal channels — and you can steer it in plain language everywhere else (“only jump in on the deploy pipeline”, or “never respond unless tagged”).

Why the old model was limited

From one message to the whole conversation

Claude Tag lets you add Claude to a Slack channel, where it responds when you @-mention it — or proactively when it thinks it can help. Before, a lightweight classifier looked at each new message on its own and made one yes-or-no call. Read in isolation, most messages aren’t for Claude, so it correctly did nothing.

The trouble is that work often lives between messages. One engineer posts a theory; another posts the evidence for it; neither message asks for anything, and nobody has a free hour to connect them. Read together, there’s an obvious task sitting there. With the classifier removed, Claude now reads across the channel — and can open a thread with the investigation already running, pulling both people in, without an @-mention.

An annoying agent is worse than an unhelpful one — so in most channels, on most messages, being helpful means saying nothing.

What it does with the context

Four moves, one of them silence

Figure 1 · How Claude Tag chooses to respond — or not
1

Reply inline

When the answer is short, verifiable, and something the channel doesn’t already know.

2

Start a thread

When a message deserves real time, it opens a thread with the work already running.

3

Route to work in flight

When a message adds to a workstream Claude already has open, the update lands there.

4

Say nothing

When nothing is called for — the most common move by far.

↻ Every move stays within the permissions, tools, and scope you configured.

Because conversations aren’t walled off from each other, an update lands in the right workstream, and when two investigations turn out to be the same bug, that connection gets made. There’s also a speed win: Claude acknowledges you in seconds instead of working silently while it spins up, so the “did it hear me?” first minute is gone.

The discipline of staying quiet

How Claude decides when not to speak

The proactivity is graded, not guessed. Claude’s channel-by-channel choices are scored against a rubric built on principles like how useful a comment is, how confident Claude is in its response, and whether there’s a person better suited to answer. That’s what keeps a more active teammate from becoming a noisy one.

Grade every choice

Usefulness of the comment, Claude’s confidence, and whether a human is better placed to respond — scored per channel, per message.

Go to sleep

In a channel where message after message it concludes it has nothing to add, Claude stops paying attention — the way people mute a channel until tagged.

Wake on mention

An @-mention wakes it instantly, even in a channel it had gone quiet in.

Steer in plain language

“Never respond here unless someone tags you” or “feel free to jump in on the deploy pipeline” — and any member can switch automatic replies off entirely.

Try it in one channel

Add Claude to a single channel and watch what it adds — and where you’d rather it stayed quiet. The update is live across Claude Tag for Teams and Enterprise, at no additional cost, with the extra context excluded from usage and spend limits.
