Context Is the Differentiator

Atlassian and OpenAI are extending a collaboration that began in 2023: OpenAI’s frontier models, including the GPT-6 family, now power agents across Atlassian’s platform and Rovo — grounded in the Teamwork Graph that links people, projects, documents and decisions.

The idea in five lines

  • A deeper partnership — OpenAI’s GPT-6 family and GPT-5.6 series now power agents across Atlassian and Rovo, extending a collaboration from 2023.
  • Grounded in context — Rovo pairs the models with Atlassian’s Teamwork Graph, an enterprise context layer connecting people, projects, documents and decisions.
  • Status becomes a question — ask whether a launch is on track and Rovo flags blockers, missed milestones and decisions needing attention from Jira and Confluence.
  • Two-way plugins — Atlassian plugins pull Jira items, Confluence content and people into ChatGPT and Codex, subject to permissions.
  • Already in use at scale — 3,000+ Atlassian developers use Codex across terminals, IDEs and code review; OpenAI uses Jira internally.
So what for you

The pattern worth copying isn’t the model — it’s the context layer. Rovo is useful because the Teamwork Graph tells the model how your company actually operates, and access stays governed by existing permissions. If you run Jira or Confluence, the near-term value is querying project status in natural language instead of assembling it by hand.

Why it matters

From understanding work to taking action

Most AI-in-the-workplace stories stop at summarizing. The Atlassian bet is that a context layer — the Teamwork Graph — lets a model reason about how a specific company operates, so a product manager can ask ‘is this launch on track?’ and get back blockers, missed milestones and decisions needing attention, drawn from real Jira tickets and Confluence docs. OpenAI’s API lead frames the goal as moving ‘from understanding their work to taking action.’
