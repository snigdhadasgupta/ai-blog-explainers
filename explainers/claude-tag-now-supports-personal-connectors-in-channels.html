Claude Tag brings your personal connectors into channels

Claude Tag can now reach the tools you personally connected — Drive, calendar, GitHub, a CRM — from inside a shared Slack channel, so one answer can combine your private sources with the channel’s, while every action stays logged under your own account.

The idea in five lines

  • Your tools, in the channel — Claude Tag can now use the connectors you personally authorized (Drive, calendar, GitHub, a CRM) during channel conversations, not just DMs.
  • Permission on first use — Claude asks before it touches a personal connector the first time in a thread, then remembers that approval for the rest of the thread.
  • Review or Auto — vet Claude’s reply before it posts, or let it post automatically unless it detects sensitive content; Enterprise admins can require Review for everyone.
  • Logged under you — everything Claude does through your connector shows up in that tool’s own log under your account, separate from the channel’s shared service account.
  • Not for unattended jobs — scheduled routines and CI/CD still run on channel-level connectors; personal connectors are for supervised, collaborative work.
So what for you

If your team runs work in Slack with Claude, personal connectors let one person pull their own Drive or GitHub context into a shared answer without handing credentials to the channel — a good moment to decide whether Review or Auto is your default, and whether admins should require Review.

What changed

From private DMs to the shared channel

Until now, the connectors you set up for yourself — your calendar, your cloud storage, your own CRM login — only worked when you talked to Claude in a direct message. In a channel, Claude was limited to the connectors the channel’s shared service account could see. That meant the richest context, the stuff tied to you, stayed out of collaborative threads.

Now Claude can use your personal connectors in a channel too. Ask it to compare your own Google Drive document against the merged pull requests from the channel’s GitHub connector, and it pulls from both — a private source only you can reach, and a shared one the team set up — then answers in the thread.

Everything Claude does through your connector appears in that tool’s own log under your account, the way your direct-message work does today.

Why it matters

Private context, without leaking credentials

The design keeps a clean line between what is yours and what is the channel’s. Your personal connector usage runs under your identity and lands in that tool’s log under your account; it does not merge into the channel’s shared service-account activity. Teammates get the benefit of your context in an answer without ever gaining access to your underlying accounts.

You also keep a hand on the wheel. Review mode lets you read Claude’s draft before it posts to the channel. Auto mode posts for you unless Claude detects something sensitive that warrants a human look. On Enterprise plans, administrators can mandate Review across the board.
