Cutting Claude Costs 50–73% Without Losing Quality

Anthropic’s platform team ran the experiments: byte-exact prompt caching, deleting outdated “be maximally thorough” instructions, and dialing effort to the task each cut cost sharply — and often lifted accuracy at the same time.

The idea in five lines

  • Three levers, not one — prompt caching, pruning legacy prompt instructions, and calibrating effort level together cut spend 50–73%.
  • Caching is near-free reuse — cache reads cost a fraction of full input pricing on byte-exact prefixes, with a 5-minute TTL extendable to an hour.
  • Old instructions cost money — removing legacy “verify twice” lines cut cost 14.6% and improved accuracy 5.3% on an Opus 4.8→5 migration.
  • Effort is a dial — Fable 5.1 at low effort matched older Fable 5 at high effort — at one-third the cost.
  • Let automation search — “/claude-api cost-optimize” cut LegalBench 58%, tau2-bench retail 73%, and SWE-bench 55%.
So what for you

Before you negotiate a bigger inference budget, audit the prompts you shipped a model or two ago — turn on prompt caching, delete instructions written for an older model, and benchmark a lower effort level. Teams routinely find half their spend was habit, not need.

The core idea

Most AI spend is habit, not need

The instinct when costs climb is to ask for more budget or a cheaper model. Anthropic’s platform team found the bigger wins came from removing waste already baked into applications: prompts carrying instructions written for older models, no caching on repeated context, and effort levels set higher than the task requires.

Removing legacy “verify twice” and “be maximally thorough” instructions during an Opus 4.8→5 migration cut cost 14.6% while improving accuracy 5.3%.

The three levers

Where the savings come from

Lever 1

Prompt caching

Cache reads cost a fraction of full input pricing when a byte-exact prefix repeats across requests. Default 5-minute TTL, extendable to an hour — manage breakpoints explicitly.

Lever 2

Prompt hygiene

Instructions tuned for a previous model become dead weight. Auditing and deleting them cut cost and lifted accuracy at once.

Lever 3

Effort calibration

Fable 5 ranged from 11.5% accuracy at low effort ($5.35/task) to 30.9% at max ($19.00). Newer Fable 5.1 at low effort matched old Fable 5 at high effort — at a third the cost.

The pattern across benchmarks: cost and accuracy are not a straight trade-off. An automated configuration search (“hillclimbing”) repeatedly found cheaper settings that also scored higher on held-out tests — because the expensive defaults were doing unnecessary work.

Your 30-day cost-optimization pass

Turn on prompt caching with explicit breakpoints, strip instructions written for a previous model, and run a benchmark sweep to find the lowest effort level that still passes — then let the automated cost-optimizer hillclimb from there.
