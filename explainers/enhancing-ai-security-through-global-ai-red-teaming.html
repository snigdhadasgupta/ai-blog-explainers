Red teaming AI, globally

Most AI safety testing still happens inside one company’s walls — but the highest-risk failure modes need multilingual, regional, and domain expertise no single internal team has. Microsoft’s External Red Team Alliance (EXTRA) funds 18 university labs on six continents and builds a distributed specialist network to close that gap.

The idea in five lines

  • Internal testing is not enough — the riskiest AI failures need domain, multilingual, and regional expertise no single company team can replicate.
  • EXTRA is the answer — Microsoft’s External Red Team Alliance is a formal global extension of its AI Red Team.
  • Two parts — unrestricted gifts to 18 university labs on six continents, plus a distributed network of specialist practitioners for hands-on red teaming.
  • Beyond prompt injection — the attack surface now spans security ops, misuse, multilingual harms, alignment failures, and domain-specific abuse.
  • Unrestricted by design — funding is not tied to product deliverables; the goal is independent safety-research capacity and better evaluation methods.
So what for you

If your organization is deploying frontier models, treat red teaming as a continuous, multi-perspective practice — not a one-time internal check. The failure modes that matter most often show up only in specific languages, regions, or domains, so budget for external and specialist review before you scale a high-stakes AI system.

Why the old model breaks

The attack surface outgrew the internal team

As frontier models get more capable, the attack surface expands with them. AI red teaming is no longer just prompt injection or content-safety edge cases — it increasingly involves security operations, misuse scenarios, multilingual harms, alignment failures, and domain-specific abuse patterns that vary sharply across geographies and languages. Microsoft’s AI Red Team concluded that meaningful testing of advanced systems requires broader participation from researchers and practitioners operating outside corporate security boundaries.

External expertise is no longer supplemental to red teaming; in many cases, it is essential.

What EXTRA is

A two-part global alliance

Part 1

A global academic network

Unrestricted gifts to 18 university labs across six continents — including Carnegie Mellon CyLab, Harvard’s Berkman Klein Center, IIT Madras CeRAI, KAIST, NYU’s Alignment Research Group, University of Toronto’s CleverHans Lab, and the University of São Paulo’s C4AI. Funding is unrestricted so researchers pursue hard, unresolved safety questions independently.

Part 2

An operational specialist network

A distributed group of practitioners and regional experts who join red teaming directly in highly specialized areas — specific attack classes, languages, cultural contexts, or technical domains that internal teams may not fully cover alone.

What the research targets

Two directions of inquiry

Attack

Securing the AI itself

Some labs examine the cybersecurity implications of AI systems — how models can be attacked, manipulated, or abused in operational environments.

Defend

AI as a defender

Others explore the inverse: how AI systems can assist defenders and improve cyber operations. The unrestricted structure is intentional — strengthen independent safety-research capacity, not predefined deliverables.

Microsoft frames EXTRA within its broader Frontier Governance Framework: combine rigorous internal governance with external support and collaboration to identify, assess, and mitigate emerging risks. The analogy is coordinated vulnerability research and responsible disclosure — global communities routinely find risks vendors alone would miss, and AI safety will need the same interdisciplinary, multilingual, globally distributed effort.

What to do about it

Build external and specialist perspectives into your own AI assurance: red team across the languages and regions you actually operate in, test misuse and alignment failures, and treat evaluation as an evolving discipline. For high-stakes deployments, one internal pass is a starting point, not a finish line.
