How Balyasny Evaluates & Governs Claude Fable 5

A $38B hedge fund doesn’t trust a new model because it tops a leaderboard. Balyasny Asset Management grades each one on thousands of its own financial tasks — then wraps the winner in a governance model where a smarter system earns no extra authority.

The idea in five lines

  • A hedge fund grades models — Balyasny Asset Management, with ~$38B under management and ~2,000 staff, runs new models through thousands of real financial tasks before trusting them.
  • Fable 5 beat the incumbent — it scored 89.4% accuracy against the prior production model’s 86.1%, and solved economics problems no earlier model had cracked.
  • Deal analysis in a day, not a week — merger-arbitrage work that took three to five days now takes less than one.
  • Thousands of agents, always on — BAM’s internal BAMAgent platform runs multi-step research agents 24/7, with teams operating 300+ at once.
  • Capability isn’t authority — safety is an operating model of least-privilege access, tool-level permissions, logging, and human review; a more capable model gets no wider reach by default.
So what for you

Before letting a more capable model loose on high-stakes work, copy BAM’s split: evaluate it on your own real tasks, not public benchmarks, and keep access, permissions, and human review decoupled from raw capability — so a model upgrade never silently widens what an agent can touch.

How they decide

Evaluation on real work, not leaderboards

Balyasny Asset Management manages roughly $38 billion with about 2,000 investment professionals and staff. Charlie Flanagan, its Chief AI Officer, runs every new model through thousands of the firm’s own real-world financial tasks — the work analysts actually do — rather than trusting a public score. Against that bar, Claude Fable 5 reached 89.4% accuracy versus 86.1% for the prior production model, and completed economics problems no previous model had solved.

The platform and the work it runs

Infrastructure

BAMAgent

An internal platform for deploying agents into approved workflows, supporting thousands of autonomous agents running around the clock with multi-step research. One tax-loss-harvesting analysis explored 90,000 database tables in a single run.

Principle

Decouple reach from smarts

Treating safety as an operating-model question — not a model property — means each upgrade is evaluated on accuracy first, then granted access deliberately. The governance stays constant even as the model underneath gets stronger.

If you’re rolling out a frontier model

Build an evaluation set from your own high-value tasks and re-run it on every model. Then separate the two questions teams tend to merge: how capable is it, and how much should it be allowed to touch? Keep least-privilege access, logging, and human review for material outputs fixed regardless of the answer to the first.
