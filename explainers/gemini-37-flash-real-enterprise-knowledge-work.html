Testing Gemini 3.7 Flash on Real Work

Box grades frontier models the way its customers use them — complex, multi-document business tasks judged on whether the finished work is correct. Gemini 3.7 Flash came out meaningfully more accurate than 3.6, and faster.

The idea in five lines

  • Graded on real deliverables — Box’s Complex Work Eval runs document-grounded tasks across a dozen industries, scored against 20–30 weighted criteria where confidently wrong answers lose points.
  • A clear step up — Gemini 3.7 Flash scored 67% to 3.6 Flash’s 62% and won a majority of head-to-head tasks, with the biggest gains on the hardest work.
  • Best where difficulty is highest — due diligence and data analysis improved most — the tasks that punish stopping early or grounding an answer in the wrong spreadsheet cell.
  • Widest gains in demanding domains — Financial Services jumped 18 points to 83%, Consumer Products hit 85%, and Legal and Healthcare each rose 12 points.
  • More accurate and faster — it finished tasks about a third quicker (73s vs 115s on average) using slightly fewer tokens and tool calls.
So what for you

When choosing a model for stakes-y knowledge work, evaluate it on your own end-to-end tasks and grade completeness and arithmetic, not vibes — and still pair it with a human reviewer for open-ended judgment calls.

How Box tests models

On the work customers actually do

Box’s Complex Work Eval is a set of realistic, document-grounded tasks spanning a dozen industries and the core modes of analytical knowledge work: analyzing data, drafting reports, running due diligence, and reviewing others’ work for errors. Each task runs end to end through an agent that must find the right documents, reason across spreadsheets, PDFs, slides, and images, and produce the deliverable a knowledge worker would hand off.

Every output is graded against 20–30 weighted criteria by an independent judge — some carry negative weight, so a confidently wrong answer is penalized, not merely unrewarded.

Where the gains show up

‘Looks thorough’ versus ‘is thorough’

The advantage grows with analytical difficulty. On a financial due-diligence task — a full year of transactions, asked to validate a vendor’s pricing tool and report every miscalculation — the weaker model reviewed the obvious cases, declared the tool error-free, and even asserted an incorrect figure. Gemini 3.7 Flash worked the full set, identified the specific wrong transactions, and made no unsupported claims.

The pattern repeats in structured review: given a contract to score item by item against a policy, 3.7 produced the correct per-item scores and total where the baseline’s tally was off. Getting the judgments and the arithmetic right, consistently, is the difference between a draft a reviewer can trust and one they redo.

Faster, and lighter to run

On the same runs, Gemini 3.7 Flash finished tasks in about a third less time — roughly 73 seconds versus 115 — while using slightly fewer tokens and tool calls. More accurate and more efficient; it will be available in Box soon.
