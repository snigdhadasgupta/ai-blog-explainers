The next measure of AI momentum is work transformed

Microsoft 365 Copilot has passed 30 million paid seats — but Microsoft’s argument is that seats were the first phase. The metric that now matters is whether the work itself changes: agents that finish multi-step workflows, tailored to how specific roles actually operate.

The idea in five lines

  • Seats were phase one — Copilot passed 30M paid seats with net adds more than doubling quarter over quarter, but Microsoft says the next phase is measured by work transformed, not licenses sold.
  • A new product shape — a model wrapped in an agentic harness that plans, executes, tests, and corrects its own work; Copilot Cowork (GA in June) runs multi-step tasks end to end.
  • Usage is going deep — conversations per user nearly doubled year over year and weekly Copilot engagement now rivals Outlook and Teams; 49% of Cowork work is multi-step analysis.
  • Enterprises see real change — Eaton, Premera Blue Cross, Levi Strauss, EY, and Microsoft’s own supply chain report cycle-time cuts, hundreds of employee-built agents, and work that couldn’t be done before.
  • Specialization beats broadcast — the biggest gains come from tailoring agents to specific roles, not handing everyone the same general-purpose tool.
So what for you

Stop scoring your AI rollout by seats and logins. Pick one role or one end-to-end workflow, simplify it before you automate it, and measure the change in the work — cycle time, tasks removed, or a capability you couldn’t staff before. That’s the number leadership will actually care about next quarter.

The thesis

From adoption to transformation

The first phase of enterprise AI was defined by adoption: deployments, licenses, and hours saved — real gains that keep compounding. Microsoft’s framing is that the next phase is defined by transformation, as AI moves from an assistant people reach for to an active participant in how work gets done. Organizations are redesigning workflows around it, and employees are directing agents to take on whole tasks and processes rather than single steps.

AI is moving beyond helping people complete tasks to helping them complete entire workflows.

That shift changes what’s worth measuring. Seat counts tell you a tool was bought; they don’t tell you whether the underlying work is faster, better, or newly possible. The rest of the post is Microsoft’s attempt to show that transformation with telemetry and named customer results.

What AI makes possible

Five enterprises, three kinds of change

Microsoft groups the customer evidence into a rough ladder: efficiency first, then better decisions, then work that couldn’t be done at all. The recurring lesson — simplify the work before you automate it, and keep experts at the center of the call.

Cycle time

Microsoft Cloud Supply Chain

Took a “lean before agents” approach — mapped and simplified six workflows, built a shared data foundation, then deployed 70+ purpose-built agents for a 75% cut in cycle time on selected workflows.

Better decisions

Eaton

An AI-powered quality agent helps experts review manufacturing quality reports to surface root causes, trends, and supplier insights — applied across ~5,000 reports, with the expert team still owning every decision.

Citizen builders

Premera Blue Cross

Put Copilot and Copilot Studio into everyday tools with training and guardrails; employees built 900+ agents, including a contract-exhibit agent that cut a 30–45 minute task to about three.

Net-new capability

Levi Strauss & Co.

An agent analyzed 1,100 finance standard operating procedures and cataloged 18,000 individual tasks in a day — sizing an opportunity a VP of Finance had assumed was impossible to quantify.

Autonomous, in the loop

EY

An Autonomous Sourcing Agent negotiates low-risk procurement across cost, delivery, and warranty with one or many suppliers — 200+ transactions since its Oct 2025 pilot, expected to reach 1,500 next year, with humans validating.

The pattern

Simplify, then automate

The teams getting the most value don’t layer agents onto broken processes. They redesign the work first, ground agents in real context, and keep human judgment on the decisions that carry weight.

The step change

A model wrapped in an agentic harness

Underneath the adoption numbers is a new product shape: not just a smarter model, but a model inside a harness that can close its own feedback loop — planning, executing, testing, and correcting until it returns a finished result. That is what powers Copilot Cowork, generally available since June: you define a multi-step task and it runs end to end.

Specialization at scale

Tailor to the role, not the org chart

Microsoft’s sharpest claim: the gap between leaders and laggards isn’t how widely they deployed AI — it’s how well they tailored it. The most common pattern is still “give everyone the same tool and let them figure it out.” The organizations creating distinct value start from how a specific role actually works, then build the agent around that.

S&P Global Commodity Insights

Expertise in the flow of work

A Copilot agent in Teams, built with Agent Builder and Graph connectors, delivers 95% faster data extraction and 98% faster comparative analysis — putting specialized market intelligence within reach of more people.

Microsoft sales

Distinct seller personas got dedicated agents (Researcher, Analyst, Sales, Deal, and a reporting agent). Customer-facing time doubled from 25% to 50%, with 9.4% more revenue per head and 20% faster deal closure.

Kantar

A system of HR agents

A “people agent” — really ~10 agents across 60 topics — turned a three-day employment-verification letter into 120 seconds, and now resolves ~40% of HR queries, up from zero in February.

Microsoft People Operations

Frontier Tuning

Rather than improving one task at a time, practitioners’ know-how was captured as tasks, skills, and quality rubrics — yielding a 5× improvement in successful task completion over a general-purpose baseline.

What to watch next

Microsoft expects the measures that matter to shift toward depth of use, measurable change in the work itself, the growth and governance of agents, and the redesign of roles and workflows. Its bet is that durable advantage goes to organizations where technology, operating models, and human agency evolve together — the “Frontier Firm” taking shape.
