The idea in five lines

  • Opus 5 is a step-change for the Opus tier: near Fable 5 intelligence at roughly half the cost per task, available today on every platform.
  • New state-of-the-art on Frontier-Bench and GDPval-AA; it more than doubles Opus 4.8 on Frontier-Bench and lands within 0.5% of Fable 5 on CursorBench at half the cost.
  • Built for daily use — the new default on Claude Max and strongest model on Claude Pro — with an effort setting to trade intelligence against token cost.
  • Stronger at verifying its own work: it writes its own test harnesses, finds true root causes, and pushes back on flawed designs.
  • Anthropic’s most aligned model yet (2.3 on the behavioral audit) and does not advance dangerous dual-use capabilities — still behind Mythos 5 on cyber exploitation and autonomous biology.
So what for you

If you run long agentic or coding workloads, re-benchmark them on Opus 5 before your next renewal — the price-for-intelligence shift may change what's worth automating.

The headline

Frontier-adjacent intelligence, Opus economics

Claude Opus 5 delivers greatly improved performance at the same price as its predecessor, Opus 4.8 — while approaching the intelligence of the frontier Fable 5 class at about half the cost. It’s designed to be used every day and works more efficiently than other models, which is why it’s the new default on Claude Max and the strongest option on Claude Pro.

What changed

It checks its own work

The recurring theme from early-access customers isn’t just raw capability — it’s judgement and thoroughness. Opus 5 verifies, iterates, and knows when not to rush.

Builds its own tools

Wrote a vision pipeline to see

Asked to rebuild a machine part in FreeCAD from a drawing it was deliberately given no way to view, Opus 5 wrote its own computer-vision pipeline to pull geometry from raw pixels — repeatedly. No competing model solved it in five attempts.

Finds root causes

Fixed what the community patch missed

On a real bug in a popular package manager, it found the root cause and fixed an edge case the community’s patch missed. A competing model patched only the surface symptom, then declared it resolved.

Long-horizon agency

Built a market data feed in one session

An engineer at a trading firm had Opus 5 build a market-data feed for a new exchange in a single session. With no live feed to validate against, it built its own test harness to confirm it parsed the data correctly.

Real judgement

Pushed back — and held its ground

In a rearchitecting session it pushed back on a proposed design, explained what was valuable in the idea, narrowed the objection to one question, and proposed a compromise — the kind of judgement that earns less oversight.

By the numbers, from early customers. Lovable: +22% on hardest agentic coding vs Opus 4.8, with far less run-to-run variance. Box: +8% overall, +11% data analysis, +17% due diligence. A legal team: similar quality at 26% fewer tokens. A trading benchmark: best-in-family using ~⅞ the reasoning tokens and under half the latency of Opus 4.8.
Alignment & safety

The most aligned Claude yet

On Anthropic’s automated behavioral audit, Opus 5 scores 2.3 on overall misaligned behavior — the lowest of its recent models. It adheres to Claude’s Constitution better than Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, or Fable 5, shows the lowest rates of deceptive behavior, is the least susceptible to being tricked into misuse, and is the safest yet at avoiding reckless, hard-to-reverse actions.

On safety, Opus 5 does not advance the frontier of risky dual-use capability. It comes close to Mythos 5 at finding cyber vulnerabilities but stays substantially behind on exploiting them (as shown on the OSS-Fuzz eval), and Mythos 5 remains stronger on long-running autonomous biology work. Its cyber classifiers are less restrictive than Fable 5’s — expected to intervene ~85% less often — allowing source-code vulnerability finding while blocking binary scanning, pen-testing, and exploit generation. Flagged requests fall back to Opus 4.8 by default.

Getting started

Available today on every platform

Opus 5 ships at $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens — the same as Opus 4.8. Developers use claude-opus-5 on the Claude API. A Fast mode runs ~2.5× the default speed at twice the base price (or via usage credits in Claude Code). Unlike the frontier tiers, Opus 5 has no data-retention requirement for general access.

Two beta updates ship alongside it: mid-conversation tool changes (swap which tools Claude can use without invalidating the prompt cache) and automatic fallbacks on the API (flagged requests route to the best available model instead of being blocked).
