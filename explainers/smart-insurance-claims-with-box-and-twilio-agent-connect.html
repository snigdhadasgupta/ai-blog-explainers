A Phone Call Becomes a Policy-Checked Claim

Box’s Harbor Home walkthrough builds a first-notice-of-loss agent: a homeowner reports damage in a natural phone conversation, and the content-heavy claims work — extraction, policy comparison, versioning, and review — happens in Box, with a human adjuster owning every decision.

The idea in five lines

  • A phone call, not a form — a homeowner reports damage in natural speech; Twilio Agent Connect runs the intake one topic at a time and confirms everyone is safe first.
  • Facts and coverage stay apart — OpenAI only extracts reported facts from the transcript — it never sees the policy and is told never to invent facts or judge coverage.
  • Box is the claim record — there’s no database: the Markdown report is the durable record, Box metadata drives the queue, and file versions track intake to enriched.
  • Box AI grounds the assessment — it compares the report and policy already living in Box and returns a conservative, cited status — likely covered, needs review, or excluded.
  • A human always decides — a Box review task routes every preliminary call to an adjuster, and any missing or malformed AI output fails safe to “Needs review.”
So what for you

When you wire AI into a regulated workflow, separate fact extraction from judgment and store the original intake before enrichment — so a claim is never lost when the model fails, and a person still owns the final decision.

The problem

First notice of loss rarely fits in a form

A homeowner dealing with water, fire, storm damage, or theft is trying to remember dates, describe affected rooms, and explain what they’ve already done to limit the damage — while a claims team needs to collect all of it, compare it with the policy, flag urgent follow-up, and route it for review. The developer’s temptation is to build yet another fragile claims-data pipeline. Box’s alternative: let the homeowner talk, and make governed content the backbone of everything downstream.

AI can organize the intake, surface relevant policy language, and suggest what to review next — but an adjuster owns the coverage decision.

The workflow

Five stages, one governed record

Figure 1 · Harbor Home first-notice-of-loss flow
1

Voice intake

Twilio Agent Connect collects the claim by phone, one topic per turn, never asking for SSN or payment data.

2

Fact extraction

OpenAI parses the transcript into a typed FNOL object — reported facts only, no coverage call.

3

Report in Box

An intake Markdown report plus operational metadata is written to Box; analysis marked pending.

4

Policy compare

Box AI compares the report and policy in place and returns a conservative, cited assessment.

5

Human review

A new file version and a Box review task route the claim to an adjuster to confirm.

There is no application database — the Markdown report is the durable claim, metadata powers the queue, and the transcript loads from Box only when opened.
Principle

Separate extraction from analysis

The model that hears the caller never judges coverage; a distinct Box AI step compares the report against the policy. Two jobs, two prompts, two schemas — each validated before it is accepted.

Principle

Fail to “Needs review”

If an AI response is missing, malformed, or unavailable, the claim stays in Box, its status becomes Needs review, and the report tells the adjuster to compare manually. Nothing silently disappears.

Principle

Store intake before enrichment

The raw report is written first, then versioned with the analysis — so a claim is preserved even when enrichment fails, and versioning shows the full progression.

Why ground it in Box

The app doesn’t rebuild what content platforms already do

Because the claim report and the policy both live in Box, the application never downloads them to build a separate retrieval pipeline — Box AI works against the governed content in place. The app still owns the caller experience, validation, orchestration, and business rules, but it doesn’t recreate content storage, version history, policy-grounded analysis, or review-task infrastructure. Box metadata doubles as the queue state, so the dashboard lists and filters claims without pulling every report.

Take it further

The demo is a starting point: add authentication and role-based access before real claim data, verify Twilio webhooks, move call state to durable storage, use a dedicated Box metadata template, and add secure uploads for photos and repair estimates. The pattern — voice in, governed content as the record, human at the decision — generalizes well beyond insurance.
