From a Two-Year Audit to a Minute

Bolt — the urban-mobility platform operating in more than 50 countries with over 4,000 employees — had 170,000+ employee documents in Box and a compliance backlog that would have taken two years to audit by hand. So it built the audit into an automated workflow instead.

The idea in five lines

  • A two-year manual audit — auditing Bolt’s 170,000+ HR documents by hand would have taken about two years on top of normal work.
  • Three Box tools, one workflow — Box Automate renames and routes files, Box Extract tags metadata, and Box Apps surfaces the structured data in a queryable dashboard.
  • One content layer — the workflow pulls documents from both Box and Workday into a single, searchable place.
  • Weeks become minutes — compliance questions that once took weeks or months are answered instantly, e.g. 20 of 55 Belgian employees have a remote-work clause.
  • People move to review — staff shift from manual checks and data entry to acting as quality reviewers over AI output.
So what for you

Bolt’s advice is the transferable part: define the rules before you automate — ‘if you’re automating a mess, you’re creating even more mess.’ Start by agreeing what a compliant document looks like and how it should be named and tagged; the automation and the instant answers follow from that structure.

Why it matters

Scale breaks manual compliance

Bolt’s problem wasn’t a lack of data — it was 170,000+ documents no team could realistically audit. A manual pass would have taken about two years while daily work continued. Automation turned an impossible audit into a running process, and changed the team’s job from opening files to reviewing output.

What to do next

Before automating a document pile, agree the rules: what a compliant file looks like, how it should be named, and which fields to extract. Then let automation build the searchable layer — and keep people as reviewers.
