Uber Puts ChatGPT Work in 30,000 Hands

After an initial pilot of 1,000 employees, Uber is rolling out ChatGPT Work to more than 30,000 people across its global workforce — its first company-wide deployment of an agentic platform built for knowledge work, with GPT-6.1 Sol as the default.

The idea in five lines

  • Company-wide, not a pilot — Uber is giving ChatGPT Work to more than 30,000 employees, up from an initial pilot of 1,000.
  • An agent, not a chatbot — ChatGPT Work is an agent inside ChatGPT Enterprise built to take on complex, multi-step tasks and deliver finished work.
  • Wired into daily tools — it connects to Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar, and Slack to research, brief, build decks, analyze data, and run cross-app workflows.
  • GPT-6.1 Sol is the default — balancing frontier performance and cost, with voice and image generation and a choice of model per task.
  • Deployment comes with scaffolding — an internal AI skills library, approved integrations, and dedicated training, aimed first at operations, finance, marketing, and product.
So what for you

Note what Uber is pairing with the license: a curated skills library, a vetted integration list, and role-specific training — not just access. A company-wide agent rollout succeeds on enablement, not seat count. If you’re planning one, budget for the skills, guardrails, and training as much as for the software itself.

What Uber deployed

An agentic platform for everyone, not a copilot for some

Uber announced a company-wide rollout of ChatGPT Work, an agent that helps employees take on a wide range of tasks within ChatGPT Enterprise. OpenAI describes it as an agentic platform designed to take on complex, multi-step tasks and deliver finished work — and calls this Uber’s first company-wide deployment of such a platform tailored for knowledge work.

Uber frames itself as a company of builders and wants to give everyone the tools to innovate, solve problems, and help shape its future — CTO Praveen Neppalli Naga.

The rollout path is the story: a 1,000-person pilot expanding to more than 30,000 employees across the global workforce, starting the day of the announcement. Uber’s developers and software engineering teams, meanwhile, keep using Codex to speed up coding workflows.

Under the hood

Models, integrations, and the enablement layer

Employees get access to OpenAI’s portfolio of frontier models, with GPT-6.1 Sol set as the default capability in ChatGPT Work for its balance of frontier performance and cost efficiency. Multimodal support — voice and image generation — is included, and teams can pick the model and tool that fit a given need.

Integrations

Where it reaches

ChatGPT Work connects with the tools Uber uses daily — Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar, and Slack — so tasks move across apps rather than ending at the chat box.

Enablement

How Uber is scaling it

An internal AI skills library, approved integrations, and dedicated employee training support the rollout, aimed first at operations, finance, marketing, and product — the layer that turns access into adoption.

OpenAI positions the expansion as another step in Uber’s broader AI strategy and its collaboration with OpenAI. President and co-founder Greg Brockman calls bringing ChatGPT Work to tens of thousands of employees a big step toward expanding access to frontier AI capabilities and tools.

The read for your own rollout

Uber’s pattern is pilot → enable → scale: prove it with 1,000, then pair company-wide access with a skills library, vetted integrations, and training. Watch for the follow-up — the adoption and outcome numbers OpenAI and Uber say they’ll publish are what will make this a case study rather than an announcement.
